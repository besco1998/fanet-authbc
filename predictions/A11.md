# A11 — other 802.11 PHYs and rates (802.11p; NR sidelink not run)

*Registered 2026-10-20 before any ns-3 run of the cells below. Plan: `docs/AUTHBC Revision
Plan.md`, A11. Outcome appended below a line at the end.*

## What is run

`ns3/authbc-links.cc` (the published scenario; at its defaults it reproduced three published
runs bit for bit, `results/raw/links_identity.csv`), with `--standard` and `--dataMode`: the
baseline (146 B, 50 frames/s) and the design (173 B, 12.5 /s) at 802.11a 6, 12, 24 Mb/s and
802.11p (10 MHz) 3, 6, 12 Mb/s, single collision domain, 30 seeds, the seven node counts per
cell in `results/raw/phy_links_predictions.csv` (`experiments/phy-links/plan.txt`); scored by
`analysis/phy_links.py --score`.

**Two things found before registering, both stated.** (1) ns-3 sends broadcast frames at
`NonUnicastMode`, which defaults to the first basic rate whatever `DataMode` says
(`WifiRemoteStationManager::GetNonUnicastMode`): without setting it, every cell would have run at
6 or 3 Mb/s under another label. The scenario sets it for every non-default PHY. (2) ns-3.48 has
no OCB MAC: 802.11p runs under the same ad-hoc, non-QoS DCF (stated substitution).

**NR sidelink is not run in this revision.** It needs the 5G-LENA NR V2X module, released against
its own ns-3 version, built separately; that is open item H6. The plan's sidelink predictions are
quoted and left unscored.

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> - On 802.11, the simulated ratio follows the Supplementary Note 7 trend (1.7× to 4.3×) and
>   grows with PHY rate. The event model predicts every crossing within 3%.
> - On NR sidelink, the gain is smaller (about 1.5–2.5×). The fixed cost there is the granularity
>   of the resource allocation rather than a preamble and backoff, and payloads are rounded up to
>   whole subchannels.

Scored as: (1a) ns-3 ratios span 1.7–4.3 and rise with rate at each channel width; (1b) every
crossing within ±3 % of the model's; (2) not scored (not run).

## Mine `[agent]`

* **A11-1.** The ratio does NOT span 1.7–4.3: it stays within **3.3–3.9** on every PHY and rate.
  The paper's "1.7–4.3" (E15) are saturation-threshold ratios of a closed form, not 0.95-delivery
  crossings; at the delivery target, the gain is set by the frame count (four times fewer) less
  the longer frame's airtime, and that varies little with rate. It does rise with rate at each
  channel width (shorter frames make the fixed cost a larger share), by less than 0.4.
* **A11-2.** Each ns-3 crossing within ±5 % of the model (the model is validated at 802.11a
  6 Mb/s only; 10 MHz timing and higher rates are new ground for it).
