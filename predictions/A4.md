# A4 — a node that chooses its batch from the channel it hears

*Registered 2026-10-20 before any run at a registered setting. Plan: `docs/AUTHBC Revision
Plan.md`, A4. Never edited after the first run; outcome appended below a line at the end.*

## Prior art, searched before this commit

ETSI's Decentralized Congestion Control (adaptive approach, derived from LIMERIC) adapts each
station's MESSAGE RATE so that the measured channel busy ratio approaches a target; LIMERIC and
ETSI Adaptive DCC are compared in Artery/Veins by an arXiv study (2403.01297). FMAC (IEEE IoT
Journal, 2021) adapts CSMA/CA to density for UAV flight-status broadcast. Adaptive message
authentication (adapting cryptographic work to observed adversaries) and batch-verification
scheduling exist for VANETs. No source found adapts the number of RECORDS PER SIGNED FRAME from
the busy ratio. What A4 adds, and all it may claim: the record rate and every record are kept,
only the frame rate changes, the age stays bounded by b_max, and the decision uses the paper's
no-fit capacity model rather than a tuned target. DCC/LIMERIC must be credited in the text.

## The controller (`ns3/authbc-adaptive.cc`)

Every second a node measures ρ̂, the share of time its radio was busy with others' frames (PHY
state trace, CCA-busy + receive), converts it from airtime to the model's airtime-plus-DIFS,
predicts the delivery of every b = 1…4 from the closed form of docs/02 §6g (W = 16, c = 8 µs,
all nodes assumed alike), and takes the smallest b whose prediction is at least 0.95 + 0.01. It
moves b one step per second, only after two seconds agree. The oracle is told how many nodes it
contends with and applies the same model to the truth, without hysteresis. Frames carry their
source, sequence, record count and the newest record's time; a frame of k records has the
length the lean codec emits (146, 155, 164, 173 B). Manoeuvre flushes are implemented and off
in the registered runs (`--manoeuvreRate 0`): no telemetry content exists in ns-3 to trigger
them, and a synthetic trigger would only add noise to the age.

## What is run (`ns3/run_adaptive.py`, scored by `analysis/adaptive_batching.py`)

Policies `adaptive`, `fixed1`, `fixed4`, `oracle`; **10 seeds** each (not 30: a ramp to 140
nodes costs up to an hour per run on this host; the scored quantities are shares of windows and
mean ages, not a crossing).

* **ramp** — one collision domain at equal power (the published channel, `FixedRss` −60 dBm),
  20 → 140 active nodes over 80 s (the plan's slope, 1.5 nodes/s; the plan's 200 is cut at 140
  because with b ≤ 4 no policy holds 0.95 above ≈ 125 nodes), 90 s.
* **merge** — two groups of 60 in 50 m discs 3 km apart on A1's channel; B is brought next to A
  at 60 s and taken away at 120 s; 180 s.
* **mobile** — A1's group mobility at 15 m/s, 100 nodes, 60 s.

## Computed before this commit (the controller's own model)

0.95-crossings of the closed form at 50 records/s: b = 1: 35.8, 2: 68.1, 3: 98.4, 4: 126.7
nodes (ns-3, published: 35.3, 66.7, 96.5, 124.6). The controller's 0.96 thresholds: it leaves

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> - Below about 35 nodes, the controller stays at b = 1 and its mean AoI matches fixed b = 1
>   within 10%. That is about 30 ms fresher than fixed b = 4, since the mean wait drops by
>   (b−1)/(2Λ).
> - At high density it reaches b = 4 and keeps V ≥ 0.95 up to about 120 nodes, within 5% of
>   fixed b = 4.
> - It meets the target at least 95% of the time in the merge scenario. Fixed b = 1 fails above
>   35 nodes, and fixed b = 4 pays the extra age at all densities.

Scored as: (1) ramp, windows with fewer than 30 nodes: adaptive mean peak age within 10 % of
fixed b = 1's; (2) the difference to fixed b = 4 at those windows, against "about 30 ms";
(3) ramp: `failure_n` (first window whose 5-window mean V < 0.95) of adaptive within 5 % of
fixed b = 4's, and adaptive's mean b ≥ 3.5 before that; (4) merge: adaptive `frac_v95` ≥ 0.95;
(5) fixed b = 1 `failure_n` ≤ 40; (6) fixed b = 4 mean peak age above adaptive's in every
scenario.

## Mine `[agent]`

* **A4-1 (age, correcting the plan's figure).** The 30 ms of the plan is the mean wait of a
  record; the peak age differs by about 1.5 × (80 − 20) = **90 ms** between fixed b = 4 and
  b = 1. Prediction: at windows with fewer than 30 nodes, fixed b = 4 is 80–100 ms older than
  adaptive.
* **A4-2 (where it steps up).** On the ramp, adaptive's mean b reaches 1.5 at 32–42 nodes, 2.5
  at 62–72, 3.5 at 90–100 (threshold plus about two seconds of hysteresis at 1.5 nodes/s, plus
  the spread across nodes).
* **A4-3 (holding the target).** Ramp: `frac_v95_n_le_115` ≥ 0.90 for adaptive and oracle,
  ≥ 0.95 for fixed b = 4. The controller lags; a smoke run on a different ramp (seed 999) kept
  every sampled window at or above 0.951, but this is not assumed.
* **A4-4 (cost of adapting).** Adaptive changes b at most 6 times per node per minute; its bytes
  per record lie between fixed b = 1's (≈ 146) and fixed b = 4's (≈ 43), lower as the run is
  denser.
* **A4-5 (merge).** Apart, each group of 60 needs b = 2 (its own crossing at b = 1 is ≈ 40 with
  capture); merged, b = 4. Adaptive's mean b over the merged minute is ≥ 3.5, apart ≤ 2.5;
  fixed b = 1 `frac_v95` < 0.5.

## Disclosure

Development runs of this scenario, none at a registered setting: two short ramps (20 → 80 nodes,
seed 7 over 20 s and seed 999 over 40 s, the second after the occupancy fix below), adaptive,
fixed b = 1 and oracle. They found one definitional error, fixed before this commit: the radio's
busy time counts airtime only, while the model's occupancy counts airtime plus DIFS; reading one
as the other made the controller switch about 10 % late (V fell to 0.93 on the first ramp). The
conversion is by the frame's own ratio, nothing fitted. On the second ramp adaptive held every
sampled window at 0.951–0.981 while fixed b = 1 fell to 0.947 at 37 nodes and 0.59 at 80.

## What this cannot show

One-hop neighbourhoods; a homogeneous-scaling assumption in the controller (others at its own
b); no telemetry content, so no manoeuvres; one PHY.

**Why the controller misses.** It does what it was registered to do — the smallest b whose
*predicted* delivery is at least 0.96 — and the realised V sits about 0.005 below that all along
the ramp (0.952–0.964 per 8-node bin), while one-second windows scatter by about ±0.01. So a
quarter of them fall below 0.95, and the 5-window rule trips at 76 nodes on average (39 to 108
across seeds). The oracle, which picks b from the true N, steps up earlier (b = 2 from 34 nodes)
and holds 0.96–0.97 to about 115. The 0.005 is lag more than model error: the controller reads
the previous period's busy time and waits two periods before moving, while the ramp adds 1.5
nodes a second; in the merge, at constant N, its merged-minute mean V (0.962) is the oracle's
(0.965), and what it loses is the few seconds after the merge. The development ramp that "held
every window" was one seed.

**What it means.** Adapting b buys freshness — 41 ms of peak age against fixed b = 4 in the
merge, 26 ms on the ramp, 9 ms in the mobile run — at the price of running at the edge of the target. As built, with a
0.01 margin, it does not keep V ≥ 0.95 in a quarter of the one-second windows of a growing
swarm; the oracle shows the gain is real when the margin is enough. A larger margin (or a
predictive term for the growth) is a new controller and a new registration — not tuned here
after seeing the result (OPEN_ITEMS H9).
