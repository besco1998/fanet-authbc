# A13 — lazy verification: a real-time tier and an evidence tier

*Registered 2026-10-20 before any timing. Plan: `docs/AUTHBC Revision Plan.md`, A13 (the plan
allows it to wait for a follow-up paper; it is registered now because A8's codec makes it
cheap). Outcome appended below a line at the end.*

## What is built and run

`authbc-rx lazy` (`hw/rust_receiver`): tier 1 checks the 8 B group tag of A7 and decodes and
rebuilds the frame — its records are usable at once (collision avoidance); tier 2 verifies the
Ed25519 signatures in batches of 64 before the records are written to the ledger. Timed per
frame on **pi-B** (open item H4), over the frames of A8's identity gate. Nodes per core by
Eq. (4) with the measured per-frame cost at 12.5 frames/s per node. Time until a record is
evidence: its fill wait (≤ 60 ms), the channel (≤ 3.5 ms), the wait for its batch (≤ 100 ms at
a 100 ms batching period) and the batch's verification. The insider case is by construction:
a holder of the group key whose frames fail their signatures has them acted on in real time
and purged from the ledger; the share acted on is the insider's share of frames.

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> with batch verification at 0.46× per signature, nodes per core roughly double compared with
> one-at-a-time verification, and exceed 500 with the compiled receiver. Records become
> evidence within 100–200 ms. The insider risk is limited to the HMAC holder, which is stated
> as a trade-off.

## Mine `[agent]`

* **A13-1.** On pi-B: tier 1 under 50 µs per frame; tier 2 0.40–0.55 of one verification per

## Outcome (2026-10-22, pi-B)

`results/hw/compiled_receiver.authbc-pi4b.csv` (`authbc-rx lazy`, five runs of 20 repetitions
over the identity gate's 10 000 frames, the same board session as A8).

| per frame | measured |
|---|---|
| tier 1: 8 B group tag, decode, rebuild (records usable) | **10.2 µs** |
| tier 2: Ed25519 in batches of 64 (records become evidence) | **117.2 µs** |
| nodes per core, Eq. (4), both tiers on one core | **628** (A8 one at a time: 289) |
| time to evidence: fill ≤ 60 ms + channel ≤ 3.5 ms + batch wait ≤ 100 ms + a batch's verification 64 × 117.2 µs = 7.5 ms | **≤ 171.0 ms** |

* Plan:
  * nodes per core roughly double with batches: **HELD** (2.17×);
  * above 500: **HELD** (628);
  * evidence within 100–200 ms: **HELD** (at most 171 ms; the bound, by the arithmetic above).
  * "The insider risk is limited to the HMAC holder": a statement of the design, not tested
    here.
* A13-1:
  * tier 1 < 50 µs: **HELD** (10.2);
  * tier 2 0.40–0.55 of one verification: **HELD** (117.2 / 269.4 = 0.435);
  * 1.7–2.3 times A8's figure and above 500: **HELD** (2.17×, 628).
* A13-2, at most 170 ms: **MISSED by 1.0 ms** (171.0 ms). The registration's own terms
  summed to 171.5 ms (60 + 3.5 + 100 + ≈ 8), so the bound I wrote was an arithmetic slip, not
  a different measurement.

**What it means.** Splitting the receiver into a fast tier and an evidence tier lets one Pi 4
core take 628 neighbours. Records are usable for collision avoidance 10 µs after a frame
arrives, and are signed evidence within 171 ms of being sampled. The cost: in the 171 ms
before its signature is checked, a record is trusted on the group tag alone. A swarm member
holding the group key could inject records that are acted on and only later purged from the
ledger.
