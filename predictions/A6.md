# A6 — the frame protocol in Tamarin

*Registered 2026-10-20 before any proof attempt. The model was only parsed and checked for
well-formedness (`tamarin-prover formal/authbc.spthy`, without `--prove`). Plan:
`docs/AUTHBC Revision Plan.md`, A6. Outcomes appended below a line at the end.*

## Model (`formal/authbc.spthy`)

Tamarin 1.12.0 with Maude 3.5.1 (release assets checked against GitHub's sha256 digests;
installed under `~/.local/opt`, not in the repository). Dolev–Yao network; a Reveal rule hands
any sender's signing key to the adversary. A sender signs ⟨'authbc', S, seq, link, data, t⟩:
a strictly increasing natural-number sequence, the hash of its previous frame, the records
(one fresh term for all b of them), and the stamp of a global clock that only advances. A
receiver verifies, accepts only a newer sequence number, keeps what it accepted, records a gap
when a number is skipped, and keeps two valid frames with one number as evidence. With
`-D=TIME` it also refuses a frame stamped more than one tick ago (the A5 rule, abstracted).

**Assumption A1, stated and not proven:** canonical encoding is injective (Tamarin's tuples are
free constructors). Supported in the code by the byte-identity tests of the canonical encoder
and the exhaustive bit-flip census of one frame.

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> stated limitations into one proof plus one demonstrated mitigation.

(Lemmas, as the plan numbers them: 1 origin and integrity — injective agreement on the frame

Expectation on effort, written down so that a failure of automation is not confused with a
failure of the property: counters and hash chains can keep Tamarin's backward search from
terminating; the plan foresaw helper lemmas. Each variant gets a budget of one hour of
automatic proof (`--prove`, default heuristic). A lemma not settled in the budget is reported
as **not settled**, never as proven, and helper lemmas added afterwards are reported as added
afterwards.

**What is by construction and what is not.** `a_skipped_frame_is_detected` follows almost
directly from the receiver's rules (a skipped number takes the gap rule); its proof says the
rules are complete, not that an adversary is defeated. `continuity_follows_the_senders_chain`,
`origin_and_integrity`, `equivocation_implies_revealed_key` and `bounded_delay_freshness` rest
on the signature and the hash and are the security content.

## What this cannot show

A symbolic model: perfect cryptography, no timing side channels, no implementation bugs; one
abstract clock (the A5 harness measures the real window with clock error). The model has one
signature scheme; Ed25519 and ECDSA P-256 are the same here.

### After registration — what made the search end (each change reported as one)

Found by bisecting on theories of a few lines (`docs/LOGBOOK.md`):

1. **A counter in a state fact keyed by a public name.** Keyed by a fresh session id instead
   (`Snd(~sid, ..)`, `Rcv(~rid, ..)`), Tamarin treats the fact as injective — one live instance
   per session — and the "counters only increase" invariants become provable; one sender and one
   receiver per name, as registered, so a session is a name. **Helper lemmas were added** (eight;
   the registration foresaw them).
2. **A receiver whose first acceptance also drew the fresh id** — the induction did not end; a
   start rule of its own (nothing accepted yet) fixed it.
3. **A clock threaded through every rule as one linear fact** — replaced, in the timed model, by
   persistent ticks whose reads are held to the latest tick by a restriction.

A model without the clock but with the chain and the registered counters
(`formal/authbc_base.spthy`, committed earlier) did not settle its first invariant within the
heap. In the first draft of the counters model (registered counters, no chain), six property
lemmas "verified" in seconds — **on top of two invariants that had not been proven** (Tamarin
assumes `[reuse]` lemmas when proving the ones after them). Those verdicts were never counted.

### Against the plan

> lemmas 1–4 are proven; lemma 5 shows the attack first and then its fix.

**HELD, across three models, two of them made after registration.** Lemma 1: counters model
(and the registered model, without TIME). Lemma 2: counters model. Lemma 3: counters model.
Lemma 4: continuity in the registered model, gap detection in the counters model. Lemma 5:
the timed model — the attack, then its absence and the freshness bound.

**What that does not prove.** That the properties hold *together* in one model with the chain
and the clock. The argument that they carry over — the counters model's receiver checks less
than the registered one (no link, no clock), so it accepts a superset of what the registered
receiver accepts, and a safety property of every acceptance survives the restriction — is an
argument, not a proof: the sender's frames differ in their fields, and Tamarin did not check the
correspondence. A reviewer may fairly ask for the one model; the attempt is
`formal/authbc_full.spthy`, and within 1 GB a lemma it did not settle.

**My effort expectation** ("counters and hash chains can keep Tamarin's backward search from
terminating; the plan foresaw helper lemmas") held, in the form it took: the counters needed
helpers and a different state encoding; the chain, with them, still did not settle in one model.
