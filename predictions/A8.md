# A8 — a compiled receiver and sender

*Registered 2026-10-20 before any timing of the compiled codec. Plan: `docs/AUTHBC Revision
Plan.md`, A8. Outcome appended below a line at the end.*

## What is built

`hw/rust_receiver/` (Rust 1.99.1; `ed25519-dalek` 2.1.1 with batch verification, `sha2` 0.10.8;
versions pinned in `Cargo.toml`): the lean frame's canonical CBOR, its record stream (LEB128,
zig-zag), the rebuild of every record and its chain link, Ed25519 verification one at a time
and in batches of 64; a command line that encodes, verifies and benchmarks.

**Byte-identity gate — already passed, before any timing** (`analysis/rust_identity.py`,
`results/raw/rust_identity.csv`): 10 000 frames of four records signed by both codecs with the
same key are identical byte for byte, and each codec verifies all 10 000 of the other's.
Sixteen of them are kept as golden vectors (`tests/vectors/rust_identity/`), held in CI by
`tests/test_rust_identity.py` through the Python codec.

## What is run — on pi-B (open item H4); this host's figures, if any, are labelled as the host's

`authbc-rx bench`: time per received four-record frame (decode, rebuild, verify), decode and
rebuild alone, batch-of-64 verification per frame; the same frames through the Python receiver
for the ratio. Nodes per core from Eq. (4) with the measured cost. Energy per record of the
compiled sender on the INA219 rig with its check passed, and a calibration against a known power
resistor and a bench multimeter (open item G22: needs Mohamed at the bench).

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> - Receive time per frame drops from 1.43 ms to at most 0.35 ms. Verification (about 259 µs,
>   already native code) then dominates.
> - One core serves at least 230 nodes, so 124 nodes need one core and **the channel binds
>   again**, as the theory says.
> - Energy per record follows the bytes: the lean format becomes cheaper than the first format.
>   This removes the anomaly from the Results.

## Mine `[agent]`

* **A8-1.** On pi-B the compiled receive time per frame lies within 0.25–0.35 ms, of which
  verification is at least 70 % (curve25519-dalek's 64-bit backend on the Cortex-A72 is close to
  the 259 µs the paper measured through `cryptography`); decode and rebuild (four SHA-256 and a
  CBOR walk) under 40 µs.
* **A8-2.** Batch-of-64 verification costs 0.40–0.55 of the single verification per frame (the
  C library measured 0.46 on pi-B).
* **A8-3.** Nodes per core with one-at-a-time verification ≥ 230; with batches ≥ 400.
* **A8-4.** Energy: not predicted beyond the plan's statement until the rig passes its check.

## Outcome, timing (2026-10-22, pi-B)

`results/hw/compiled_receiver.authbc-pi4b.csv` (`analysis/compiled_receiver_bench.py`,
committed at 2719fba before the run). pi-B: 1.8 GHz, `performance` governor, throttling flags
0x0 before and after, 49.6 → 55.5 °C. The identity gate's 10 000 frames (first 16 checked
against the golden vectors). rustc 1.99.1, the host's.

| per four-record frame | Rust (5 runs × 20 reps, median [range]) | Python |
|---|---|---|
| receive: decode, rebuild, verify | **276.7 µs** [276.3, 277.1] | **1 408.7 µs** [1 408.3, 1 409.1] |
| decode and rebuild alone | 7.3 µs | — |
| verification, batch of 64, per frame | 117.1 µs | — |
| nodes per core, Eq. (4), 12.5 frames/s | **289** (one at a time), **643** (batches of 64) | **57** |

The Python figure reproduces the paper's 1.43 ms on the same board to 1.5 %. Measured on the
same board the same day (`flood_cost.authbc-pi4b.csv`): one Ed25519 verification through
`cryptography` takes 262.6 µs; through `ed25519-dalek`, 269.4 µs (receive minus decode).

* Plan (1):
  * receive at most 0.35 ms: **HELD** (0.277 ms);
  * verification dominates: **HELD** (97 % of the compiled receive; about 269 µs against the
    plan's "about 259").
* Plan (2):
  * at least 230 nodes per core: **HELD** (289);
  * 124 nodes then need one core: **HELD** (0.43 of one);
  * the channel binds again: **HELD** (the channel's crossing is 124.55 nodes, below the core's
    289).
* Plan (3), energy follows the bytes: **NOT RUN** — waits for the rig's calibration (G22,
  Mohamed at the bench).
* A8-1:
  * 0.25–0.35 ms: **HELD**;
  * verification ≥ 70 %: **HELD** (97 %);
  * decode and rebuild < 40 µs: **HELD** (7.3 µs).
* A8-2, batch of 64 at 0.40–0.55 of a single verification: **HELD** (0.435; the C library
  measured 0.46).
* A8-3, ≥ 230 nodes per core singly and ≥ 400 with batches: **HELD** (289, 643).

**What it means.** The interpreter, not the signature, was what made the receiver need three
cores for 124 neighbours (F84): the same frames in compiled code take a fifth of the time, and
one core serves 289 neighbours, or 643 when signatures are checked 64 at a time. The verdict
"124 nodes need about 3 cores" belongs to the Python prototype; with a compiled receiver the
radio channel is the limit again, as the capacity analysis assumes.
