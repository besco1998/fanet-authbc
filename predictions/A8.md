# A8 — a compiled receiver and sender

*Registered 2026-10-20 before any timing of the compiled codec. Plan: `docs/AUTHBC Revision
Plan.md`, A8. Outcome appended below a line at the end.*

## What is built

`hw/rust_receiver/` (Rust 1.99.1; `ed25519-dalek` 2.1.1 with batch verification, `sha2` 0.10.8;
versions pinned in `Cargo.toml`): the lean frame's canonical CBOR, its record stream (LEB128,
zig-zag), the rebuild of every record and its chain link, Ed25519 verification one at a time
and in batches of 64; a command line that encodes, verifies and benchmarks.

**Byte-identity gate — already passed, before any timing** (`analysis/rust_identity.py`,
`results/raw/rust_identity.csv`): 10 000 frames of four records signed by both codecs with the
same key are identical byte for byte, and each codec verifies all 10 000 of the other's.
Sixteen of them are kept as golden vectors (`tests/vectors/rust_identity/`), held in CI by
`tests/test_rust_identity.py` through the Python codec.

## What is run — on pi-B (open item H4); this host's figures, if any, are labelled as the host's

`authbc-rx bench`: time per received four-record frame (decode, rebuild, verify), decode and
rebuild alone, batch-of-64 verification per frame; the same frames through the Python receiver
for the ratio. Nodes per core from Eq. (4) with the measured cost. Energy per record of the
compiled sender on the INA219 rig with its check passed, and a calibration against a known power
resistor and a bench multimeter (open item G22: needs Mohamed at the bench).

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> - Receive time per frame drops from 1.43 ms to at most 0.35 ms. Verification (about 259 µs,
>   already native code) then dominates.
> - One core serves at least 230 nodes, so 124 nodes need one core and **the channel binds
>   again**, as the theory says.
> - Energy per record follows the bytes: the lean format becomes cheaper than the first format.
>   This removes the anomaly from the Results.

## Mine `[agent]`

* **A8-1.** On pi-B the compiled receive time per frame lies within 0.25–0.35 ms, of which
  verification is at least 70 % (curve25519-dalek's 64-bit backend on the Cortex-A72 is close to
  the 259 µs the paper measured through `cryptography`); decode and rebuild (four SHA-256 and a
  CBOR walk) under 40 µs.
* **A8-2.** Batch-of-64 verification costs 0.40–0.55 of the single verification per frame (the
  C library measured 0.46 on pi-B).
* **A8-3.** Nodes per core with one-at-a-time verification ≥ 230; with batches ≥ 400.
* **A8-4.** Energy: not predicted beyond the plan's statement until the rig passes its check.

`results/hw/compiled_receiver.authbc-pi4b.csv` (`analysis/compiled_receiver_bench.py`,
committed at 2719fba before the run). pi-B: 1.8 GHz, `performance` governor, throttling flags
0x0 before and after, 49.6 → 55.5 °C. The identity gate's 10 000 frames (first 16 checked
against the golden vectors). rustc 1.99.1, the host's.

| per four-record frame | Rust (5 runs × 20 reps, median [range]) | Python |
|---|---|---|
| receive: decode, rebuild, verify | **276.7 µs** [276.3, 277.1] | **1 408.7 µs** [1 408.3, 1 409.1] |
| decode and rebuild alone | 7.3 µs | — |
| verification, batch of 64, per frame | 117.1 µs | — |
| nodes per core, Eq. (4), 12.5 frames/s | **289** (one at a time), **643** (batches of 64) | **57** |

The Python figure reproduces the paper's 1.43 ms on the same board to 1.5 %. Measured on the
same board the same day (`flood_cost.authbc-pi4b.csv`): one Ed25519 verification through
`cryptography` takes 262.6 µs; through `ed25519-dalek`, 269.4 µs (receive minus decode).

* Plan (1):
  * receive at most 0.35 ms: **HELD** (0.277 ms);
  * verification dominates: **HELD** (97 % of the compiled receive; about 269 µs against the
    plan's "about 259").
* Plan (2):
  * at least 230 nodes per core: **HELD** (289);
  * 124 nodes then need one core: **HELD** (0.43 of one);
  * the channel binds again: **HELD** (the channel's crossing is 124.55 nodes, below the core's
    289).
* Plan (3), energy follows the bytes: **NOT RUN** — waits for the rig's calibration (G22,
  Mohamed at the bench).
* A8-1:
  * 0.25–0.35 ms: **HELD**;
  * verification ≥ 70 %: **HELD** (97 %);
  * decode and rebuild < 40 µs: **HELD** (7.3 µs).
* A8-2, batch of 64 at 0.40–0.55 of a single verification: **HELD** (0.435; the C library
  measured 0.46).
* A8-3, ≥ 230 nodes per core singly and ≥ 400 with batches: **HELD** (289, 643).

**What it means.** The interpreter, not the signature, was what made the receiver need three
cores for 124 neighbours (F84): the same frames in compiled code take a fifth of the time, and
one core serves 289 neighbours, or 643 when signatures are checked 64 at a time. The verdict
"124 nodes need about 3 cores" belongs to the Python prototype; with a compiled receiver the
radio channel is the limit again, as the capacity analysis assumes.

## Energy — registered 2026-10-22 before any energy run of the compiled sender

**Decision (Mohamed, 2026-10-22):** assume the INA219 is calibrated. G22 (a reference load) is
waived, not done. Every µJ below carries the sensor's unmeasured gain, as July's and October's
figures do. Ratios between rows of one sensor do not depend on it.

**Rig:** `authbc-pi4b`, meter channel 2, `RIG CHECK PASSED` live and against the board's own
record at 17:01 and 17:02 UTC (`results/hw/energy/rig/rigcheck-20261022T170113Z.json`,
`…T170219Z.json`): supply 5.19 V with one core busy, 5.10 V with all four, no under-voltage.

**What runs** (`hw/validate_energy_e2e.py`, as committed at a8bef6c; 60 s idle / 60 s load × 5;
one capture per run; nobody logs in to the board while it runs):

* **C:** October's control repeated exactly: first format, CBOR, every record signed (`--batch 1
  --encoding cbor --record-bytes 107 --t-enc-ns 50407 --p-cpu-w 0.749`).
* **R4 and R1:** the compiled sender, `--format rust`. `authbc-rx sendloop`, one process per
  load window, checks the chain, encodes and signs, on the Python lean rows' own records and
  key, with four and one records to a frame.

**Timing, measured on the board before this registration** (`authbc-rx sendbench`, three runs
of 20 repetitions, `results/hw/energy/e2e_2026-10-22/sendbench.authbc-pi4b.log`): **103.4 µs**
per four-record frame, **93.8 µs** per one-record frame. Python's lean sender: 868 and 379 µs.

| run | expected µJ per record | added power |
|---|---|---|
| C | 113–125 (October's registered range; October metered 116.2) | 0.70–0.80 W |
| R4 | 0.749 W × 103.4 µs / 4 = **19.4**; within 10 %: **17.4–21.3** | 0.70–0.85 W |
| R1 | 0.749 W × 93.8 µs = **70.3**; within 10 %: **63.2–77.3** | 0.70–0.85 W |

The power range for compiled code is wider because a compiled loop's power is unmeasured; the
rig check's busy core added 0.80 W. Batching saves **60–80 %** of the compiled sender's energy
per record (72 % by timing).

**Scoring of plan (3)**, "energy per record follows the bytes: the lean format becomes cheaper
than the first format": **HELD** if both hold:

* R4's median is below 58.4 µJ, the first format with one signature per four records (July,
  `energy_table.csv`);
* R1's median is below C's median: the first format with every record signed, on the same
  board and sensor, the same day.

The comparison is the compiled lean sender against the first format's sender as metered (in
Python). No compiled first format exists, and the plan names the lean format's compiled sender.

**If C is outside 113–125 µJ, stop:** the sensor or board is not in October's state, and no
figure of today stands beside October's or July's.

## Outcome, energy (2026-10-22, pi-B on the rig)

`results/raw/energy_runs_2026-10-22.csv` (`analysis/energy_runs.py --session 2026-10-22`); the
captures, manifests, logs and the board's state before and after each run are in
`results/hw/energy/e2e_2026-10-22/`.

* Every window was clean: flags 0x0, no under-voltage event, 48.7–55.0 °C.
* All 5 repetitions of each run were kept by the idle rule.
* Nobody logged in to the board during a run.
* The captures dropped 6, 34 and 4 malformed serial lines (0.02–0.1 % of the samples) while
  SIH flights loaded the host. Sample losses that do not depend on the window's state leave a
  window's mean power unchanged.

| run | µJ per record, median [range] | added power | registered | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C, first format, every record signed | **118.0** [117.0, 118.2] | 0.747 W | 113–125, 0.70–0.80 W | **inside** (October: 116.2) |
| R4, compiled lean, 4 records a frame | **17.1** [16.9, 17.1] | 0.660 W | 17.4–21.3, 0.70–0.85 W | **outside, below** (both) |
| R1, compiled lean, 1 record a frame | **60.7** [60.2, 62.4] | 0.646 W | 63.2–77.3, 0.70–0.85 W | **outside, below** (both) |

* **Plan (3), energy per record follows the bytes, the lean format cheaper than the first
  format: HELD.**
  * R4, 17.1 µJ, is below the first format's 58.4 with one signature per four records: 3.4
    times less.
  * R1, 60.7 µJ, is below C's 118.0 with every record signed: 1.9 times less.
  * The anomaly of the Results, Python's lean sender at 2.7 times the first format's energy,
    belongs to the interpreter.
* **My registered ranges: MISSED, both below.** The rate was as timed: 38 600 records/s at
  b = 4 is 103.6 µs a frame, against `sendbench`'s 103.4. The power was not. The compiled loop
  adds 0.65–0.66 W against the 0.749 W measured for Python's, 12–14 % less, and the energy
  follows: 0.660 W × 103.6 µs / 4 = 17.1 µJ.
* Batching saves 60–80 % of the compiled sender's energy per record: **HELD** (72 %).

**What it means.** Compiled, the design's sender spends 17 µJ per record on the Pi 4, a ninth
of what the Python prototype spent (155 µJ) and a third of the first format's. The paper's
"the design that is smallest on air is not the cheapest to compute" was a property of the
prototype. Compiled code also draws less power per busy core than the interpreter, so
energy models that charge one power figure for all code overstate compiled work by 12–14 %.

**Caveat (Mohamed's decision):** the sensor's gain is assumed, not calibrated (G22 waived). It
scales every µJ alike and leaves the ratios unchanged.
