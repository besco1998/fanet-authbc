# A9 — richer records, three airframes, 50 Hz flights

*Registered 2026-10-20 before any registered flight. Plan: `docs/AUTHBC Revision Plan.md`, A9.
Outcome appended below a line at the end.*

## What is run

* **Flights.** PX4 v1.17.0 software-in-the-loop with its built-in simulator SIH (no Gazebo):
  `sihsim_quadx`, `sihsim_airplane` (fixed wing), `sihsim_standard_vtol`; **missions 1–10 of
  each**, `analysis/px4_airframe_flight.py`. A mission is a closed polygon of 4–6 legs whose
  size, heading and commanded speed are drawn from a generator seeded with (airframe, number):
  multicopter 5–15 m/s and 150–400 m legs, fixed wing and VTOL 15–25 m/s (VTOL 15–22) and
  600–1 500 m legs; uploaded over MAVLink and flown in AUTO.MISSION, ended at the last waypoint.
  Logged at 20 ms: position, attitude, body rates, GNSS, airspeed, wind, mission progress,
  battery, status, thrust. Fixed-wing settings needed in SIH: no landing pattern required
  (`MIS_TKO_LAND_REQ 0`), no airspeed sensor (`SYS_HAS_NUM_ASPD 0`).
* **Records.** `analysis/record_size_sensitivity.py`: in-flight records on the 20 ms grid with 9
  (the paper's), 15 (+ attitude, body rates) and 25 fields (+ GNSS fix type, satellites,
  accuracies, airspeed, wind, mission item, a status word, thrust), coded as the lean format
  codes its own (keyframe varints, zig-zag deltas), frames of b = 1–4 at their exact lengths,
  timestamps placed an hour into a flight (the sizing convention's origin). On nine fields the
  code reproduces the encoder: the same record-stream bytes and the same frame lengths as
  `wire_v2` encodes (`tests/test_record_size_sensitivity.py`).
  **Stated difference from the plan:** its 15-field set lists GNSS fix quality too, which makes
  16; fix quality is moved to the 25-field set.
* **Capacity** by the no-fit closed form (docs/02 §6g; within 5.5 % of the eighteen simulated
  crossings), baseline at 50 frames/s, design at 12.5. The plan names Eq. (6); the closed form
  is used because Eq. (6) left its band for frames outside its fitted range in A15.
* The plan's "public Flight Review logs faster than 5 Hz": none is added — the pinned selection
  rule of the twelve logs already in the paper found none, and widening it now would be a rule
  chosen after looking.

### Why the deltas are the field count

The lean format writes every field in every delta, as a zig-zag varint, and a varint holds a
change of up to ±63 units in one byte. Over 20 ms that is:

* position: 35 m/s north–south (1e-7° of latitude ≈ 1.1 cm) and 23.7 m/s east–west at the
  missions' 47.4° N;
* altitude rate: 31.5 m/s;
* change of velocity: 31.5 m/s².

So the delta costs one byte per field for every airframe here: 9.0, 16.0 and 26.0 B. What goes
above one byte is the roll and pitch rates (mrad/s, 1.3–1.6 B each) and, under hold, a doubled
position step.

From the encoding, not from a flight: with samples 20 ms apart, at the registered 25 m/s only
longitude could need a second byte, so the 9-field delta stays below 10 B. Plan (3)'s 10–13 B was out of reach at any
registered speed.

The keyframe is smaller than the plan's ~60 B because most added fields are one- or two-byte
varints.

### Sensitivity check, added after scoring: the resampling

The logs' samples alternate 16 and 24 ms apart. Holding them on the 20 ms grid (the registered
method) repeats every other value and doubles the next step. A real sender ticking at 50 Hz
against such an estimator would do the same.

`--nearest` takes each topic's nearest sample instead (`results/raw/record_size_nearest.csv`;
tests in `tests/test_record_size_sensitivity.py`). The effects:

* keyframes: changed by ≤ 0.014 B;
* ratios: changed by ≤ 0.007;
* savings: changed by ≤ 0.03 points;
* the fixed-wing and VTOL **9-field deltas: 9.000 B, equal to the quadrotor's**. A9-3's 9-field
  margin was the doubled steps of the hold. At 15 and 25 fields the fixed wing stays above by
  0.04–0.05 B and the VTOL by 0.01 B.

### What this cannot show

* **GNSS fields are constant in SIH** (fix 3, 10 satellites, eph 0.90 m, epv 1.78 m) and so is
  the status word. In real flight they vary slowly. A delta's byte for them is unchanged (any
  change up to ±63 costs one byte). The keyframe could grow by about 1 B (eph above 1.27 m).
* SIH has no sensor noise of a real airframe. Position steps are smooth.
* The fixed wing flew at 15 m/s, not 25.

**What it means.** The paper's 9-field record is not a best case for the delta. Adding attitude,
rates, GNSS quality, airspeed, wind, mission and thrust (25 fields) lowers the saving from 70.1 %
to 63.4 % and the capacity ratio from 3.5 to 3.05. Between airframes the delta differs by at
most 0.1 B, the keyframe by 1.2 B, the ratio by 0.016. Batching's gain is set by the frame count: the ratio stays above 2 until a
record's delta reaches 159–282 B.

## Addendum (2026-10-22, after the outcome): why the fixed wing stopped near 14 m/s, and whether speed matters

Asked by Mohamed. `analysis/sih_speed_limit.py` → `results/raw/sih_speed_limit.csv` (every
fixed-wing and VTOL log, with its SHA-256) and `results/raw/record_size_speed.csv`.

**A correction to the outcome above.** "Fixed wing and VTOL cruise 15 m/s" is the *commanded*
speed. Neither reached it:

| aircraft | level-flight airspeed | throttle there |
|---|---|---|
| airplane | 13.0–13.9 m/s (most flights 13.8–13.9; highest 14.9) | 91–100 % |
| VTOL | 12.8 m/s | 100 % |

Fixed-wing ground speeds stay as given (11.9 and 11.2 m/s means).

**Why.** SIH's airplane has 6 N of thrust (`SIH_T_MAX`), and the VTOL's pusher has 4 N
(2 × `SIH_T_MAX`). On top of the drag of its aerodynamic surfaces, SIH subtracts a linear
damping force `SIH_KDV · v` (`sih.cpp`, `generate_fw_aerodynamics`). PX4 documents that term as
a "first order drag coefficient … the greater this value, the slower the quad will move"; the
airframe sets 0.2 N per m/s, which is 2.8 N at 14 m/s. Thrust equals drag near 14 m/s. Told to
fly faster, the controller trades altitude for speed and circles waypoints (the failed attempts
at 19.1 and 20.2 m/s).

**Causal check** — three diagnostic flights of one new mission (98, not among 1–10), all
commanded to 22 m/s, one SIH parameter changed at a time (`--param`):

| flight | change | level airspeed | max | completed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| diag-ctrl | none | 10.4 m/s | 13.6 m/s | no (20 min) |
| diag-thrust | `SIH_T_MAX` 6 → 12 N | 21.3 m/s | 22.7 m/s | yes |
| diag-kdv | `SIH_KDV` 0.2 → 0 | 20.9 m/s | 21.8 m/s | yes |

Removing the damping term alone lets the stock airplane fly 21 m/s. **The limit is a property of
PX4's simplified SIH model, not of fixed-wing aircraft.**

**Does speed change the result?** Both 21 m/s flights flew about 18 m/s average ground speed,
against 11.8–12.3 m/s for the registered flights:

| | registered (airplane-01, -05) | diagnostic (thrust, kdv) |
|---|---|---|
| 9-field delta, nearest / hold | 9.00 / 9.00–9.17 B | 9.09 / 9.33 B |
| saving at 9 fields | 70.07–70.17 % | 70.06–70.15 % |
| capacity ratio at 9 fields | 3.503 | 3.503 |
| 25-field delta / ratio | 26.0–26.2 B / 3.03–3.05 | 26.2–26.4 B / 3.058 |

At 50 % more speed the delta grows by 0.1–0.3 B and the ratio does not move. From the encoding,
not a flight: at 25 m/s only the longitude field can need a second byte (above 23.7 m/s
east–west), so the 9-field delta stays below 10 B.

* Plan (3)'s 10–13 B stays **missed**.
* A9-3, which tied at 9 fields under the nearest sample, shows a 0.09 B fixed-wing excess at
  18 m/s ground speed.

None of the outcome's conclusions changes. The diagnostic flights are one mission each, flown
after the outcome, and are reported as evidence about speed, not as registered data.

## Addendum 2 (2026-10-22, evening): the airplane flew part of its first leg at ground level

**Found** while choosing the SIH profile for the re-fly (A9-fast). After its takeoff item, every
registered airplane flight dived back to ground level, skimmed the ground at cruise speed for
80–100 s, and climbed again as it neared waypoint 1. GPS and the global altitude agree. The land
detector read "airborne" throughout, so those records are in the outcome's sizes. The VTOL
flights did not do this.

**Cause** (corrected the same evening). PX4's fixed-wing energy controller (TECS) follows a
reference altitude that moves at 3–5 m/s toward the target. TECS runs from start-up in every
mode that controls position, and re-initialises its reference only after a gap of more than 1 s
in its updates. In SIH its first update read 0 m above sea level, before the estimator had an
altitude, so the reference climbed from sea level. After the runway takeoff the airplane
followed a reference still about 300 m under the airfield. It pitched down 30°, cut throttle,
and skimmed the ground until the reference rose past the airfield.

Seen directly in a diagnostic flight with the setpoints logged (`dev-plane-stock-setpoints`,
`tecs_status.altitude_reference`):

| time | TECS reference | airplane |
|---|---|---|
| 40 s | −295 m | 81 m |
| 64 s | −223 m | on the ground |
| 140 s | +5 m | climbs |

*First attributed, wrongly, to the altitude first-order hold of
`FixedWingModeManager::control_auto_position`. The waypoint added against that did not stop the
descent, and a 25 m/s airplane circled it for 15 minutes; it is removed.* The re-fly spends
three seconds in manual mode before the mission (`reset_tecs`). The VTOL's takeoff is not
under TECS, which is why its flights were clean.

**Effect on the outcome** — `record_size_sensitivity.py --min-alt 20` drops every record below
20 m above home (`results/raw/record_size_airborne.csv`):

| | records dropped | 9-field delta | saving | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| airplane | 25.6 % | 9.057 → 9.072 B | 70.16 → 70.15 % | 3.519 → 3.524 |
| quadrotor | 9.0 % (climb and descent below 20 m) | 9.000 → 9.000 | 70.13 → 70.13 | 3.503 → 3.503 |
| VTOL | 3.4 % | 9.067 → 9.068 | 70.10 → 70.10 | 3.503 → 3.503 |
| all 30, 25 fields | | 26.022 → 26.026 B | 63.35 → 63.35 % | 3.049 → 3.049 |

**No conclusion of the outcome changes**; the largest change is 0.02 B. The first addendum's
two diagnostic flights (diag-thrust, diag-kdv) had the same ground segment. Their speed
comparison is repeated on clean flights by A9-fast.
