# A3 — the verifiability margin

*Registered 2026-10-19, before any run of `analysis/verifiability_margin.py` at a registered
setting. Plan: `docs/AUTHBC Revision Plan.md`, A3. Never edited after the first run; outcomes
are appended below a line at the end.*

## Why

The design meets V ≥ 0.95 with no margin: 0.952 [0.947, 0.957] at p = 0.05, and 0.947 under
length-dependent loss. The specification ties the target to the loss (ε = p); a deployment
needs a rule for the target given the loss it expects.

## What is run

`analysis/verifiability_margin.py --run` → `results/raw/verifiability_margin.csv`. The codec in
the loop exactly as the paper's loss table (E10): real lean frames from `LeanSender` with one
self-contained frame in r, a loss process, `LeanReceiver`; a frame counts only if decoded,
verified and stored; 30 seeds × 240 frames per cell; bootstrap interval of the mean.

* p ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.03, 0.05, 0.08} × b ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4} × r ∈ {1, 2, 4}, Λ = 50.
* Loss: independent; Gilbert–Elliott with the same mean and mean bursts of 2, 4, 8 frames;
  length-dependent (one bit error rate, the one that loses the one-record frame with p, so a
  longer frame is lost more often).
* ε ∈ {0.05, 0.08, 0.10} applied in the analysis: ε moves the target, not the run.
* **Trace-driven loss (part 2, after A1 stage 2).** Per-link success sequences exported from
  A1's ns-3 runs (S1 at R = 1000 m and S3 at 15 m/s, design configuration, at the stage-2 grid
  point nearest the design's crossing, 50 neighbour pairs per run, 30 seeds) replace the loss
  process; everything else as above. The export is an option of the spatial scenario added
  after A1's runs, absent by default; its registration is this paragraph.

## Computed before this registration — arithmetic, labelled so

* At r = 1 every frame verifies alone, so the expected V is 1 − p_frame **for every loss
  process**, bursty or not: burstiness changes the spread across seeds, not the mean.
* Length-dependent loss at p = 0.05: the frames of b = 1, 2, 3, 4 (145.8, 155.0, 164.0,
  173.0 B) are lost with 0.0500, 0.0525, 0.0549, 0.0573, so V = 0.950, 0.948, 0.945, 0.943;
  the tolerable one-record-frame loss for V = 0.95 is 0.0500, 0.0476, 0.0455, 0.0436.
* Eq. (3) and its Gilbert form give, at p = 0.05: r = 4 independent 0.881, Gilbert (4) 0.931.

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> - r = 1 passes with margin whenever ε ≥ p + 0.02. Example: p = 0.05 and ε = 0.08 gives
>   V ≈ 0.95, which is 0.03 above the target.
> - Under length-dependent loss, the tolerable baseline-frame loss is about 0.044 at b = 4 (as
>   in the paper) and about 0.047 at b = 2.
> - Trace-driven V lies between the independent and Gilbert values, because ns-3 collision loss
>   is mildly bursty.

Scored as: (1) at r = 1, every cell with ε ≥ p + 0.02 has V_meas ≥ 1 − ε, and its bootstrap
interval's lower end ≥ 1 − ε, for all four loss processes of equal mean (independent and the
three Gilbert); (2) the tolerable loss, read by linear interpolation of V_meas(p) at r = 1
under length-dependent loss, is within ±0.003 of 0.044 at b = 4 and 0.047 at b = 2; (3) at
r = 2 and r = 4, V_trace lies between V_iid(p̂) and V_gilbert8(p̂), p̂ the trace's own mean loss.

## Predictions — mine `[agent]`

* **A3-1.** At r = 1, V_meas lies within its bootstrap interval of 1 − p_frame in every cell
  of every loss process, and the Gilbert cells' intervals are wider than the independent ones
  (burst 8 widest).
* **A3-2.** At r > 1 every measured V lies within ±0.01 of Eq. (3) or its Gilbert form, and for
  equal mean the Gilbert value exceeds the independent one, more so for longer bursts.
* **A3-3 (the rule the plan proposes).** "Set ε at least 0.02 above the expected frame loss"
  holds for r = 1 under every process of this sweep **including** length-dependent loss up to
  b = 4 (the length penalty is at most 0.0073 at p = 0.05, well under 0.02). It does not hold
  for r > 1 under independent loss: at p = 0.05, r = 2 gives 0.926 < 0.93.
* **A3-4 (trace).** The trace's mean loss at the design's crossing is 0.05 ± 0.01 (that is how
  the crossing is defined), its V at r = 1 equals 1 − p̂ within its interval, and its mean burst
  is below 2 frames — collisions in a contended channel are close to independent from one
  frame to the next of the same sender.

## Disclosure

No run of this script exists. The expectations above are arithmetic on frame lengths and the
model functions of `authbc.models.frame`, computed today before writing this.

## What this cannot show

The loss processes are synthetic except part 2, whose traces are ns-3's. No air-to-air loss
is measured (that is B4).

**Found while scoring — the interval method undercovers (finding F86).** The theory lay inside
the bootstrap interval in 179 of 200 r > 1 cells (89.5 %), not ≈ 95 %. Checked directly, with a
known mean: the repository's interval (`bootstrap_ci`, percentile, mean, 30 values) covers
**93.7 %** for binomial per-seed values at p = 0.05 and **91.8 %** for overdispersed ones; a
t-interval covers 95.3 %. Cells with r > 1 are overdispersed (a loss propagates through its
group). Every V interval in the paper uses this method at 30 seeds; they are slightly narrow.
Nothing published is changed; open item H2.

**What the run says for deployment.** For r = 1 — the design — setting ε at least 0.02 above
the expected frame loss keeps the MEAN V at or above target under every loss process tried,
including length-dependent loss up to four records a frame. It does not give a 95 % interval
above target at 7 200 frames when losses come in bursts of four or more; there a margin of
0.03 does (no failing cell has ε − p ≥ 0.03). For r > 1 the rule fails in expectation.
