# A7 — flooding: what a forged frame costs a receiver, and what stops it

*Registered 2026-10-20 before any timing of the paths below. Plan: `docs/AUTHBC Revision
Plan.md`, A7. Outcome appended below a line at the end.*

## What is built

`src/authbc/placement/admission.py` (tests `tests/unit/placement/test_admission.py`): a layer
in front of the timed receiver, applying in order an optional 8 B group HMAC envelope (zero
change to the frame format: the tag wraps it), cheap checks (length, CBOR shape, known sender,
sequence window), a per-sender token bucket, then `LeanReceiver`. The plan's worst-case attack
frame — well formed, naming a real sender, a newer sequence number, a wrong signature — is held
by a test to pass every cheap check and cost a verification.

## What is run — on pi-B (open item H4); a quiet-host run is reported separately as the host's

The plan's design: the existing receiver fed through a local UDP socket, radio out of the loop;
honest load 124 senders × 12.5 frames/s; attack 0–3 000 frames/s; mitigations: none, cheap
checks, per-sender cap, group HMAC, and Ed25519 batch verification (64 per batch; the Python
library has no batch verification, so this row uses the C timing of `hw/ed25519_batch`, which
measured 0.46 of the per-signature cost on pi-B). Metrics: per-frame cost of each path, the
receiver's core share, honest V against attack rate, the attack rate at which honest V falls
below 0.95. Harness: `analysis/flood_cost.py` (written before the run, committed with its
outcome; the paths it times are the ones the unit tests define).

## Computed before this registration

The 8 B tag lengthens the design's frame from 173 to 181 B: the no-fit closed form's capacity
falls from 126.7 to 122.8 nodes (−3.1 %), Eq. (6)'s from 125.0 to 121.8 (−2.6 %).

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> - With no mitigation, about 700 fake frames/s saturate one core of the Python receiver
>   (1.43 ms per frame).
> - With the group HMAC, an outsider needs more than 100 000 frames/s to saturate a core, which
>   the channel cannot carry. Outsider flooding is effectively removed.
> - An insider holding a valid swarm key remains a threat; the per-sender cap limits that damage
>   to the sender being impersonated.
> - The HMAC's 2 bytes per record reduce Nmax by about 2% (by equation 6).

## Mine `[agent]`

* **A7-1.** A forged frame costs within 10 % of an honest one (it is decoded, rebuilt and
  verified alike; it is refused only at the signature): ≈ 1.4 ms on pi-B, so one core saturates
  at ≈ 700 forged frames/s, as the plan says.
* **A7-2.** The HMAC check costs under 20 µs per frame in Python on pi-B (one HMAC-SHA256 over
  ~180 B plus the call): an outsider needs > 50 000 frames/s per core — more than the channel
  carries (a 181 B frame takes ≈ 0.35 ms on air: < 3 000 frames/s). Held: "effectively removed".
* **A7-3.** The tag costs 3 % of capacity by the no-fit closed form (computed above), not 2 %:
  the plan's figure is Eq. (6) with a 2 B-per-record rounding.
* **A7-4.** Cheap checks alone stop nothing of the worst-case frame (by construction it passes
  them); they stop replays and garbage at a parse's cost (≈ 0.1 ms in Python).

| honest, + cheap checks / + per-sender cap / + group tag | 1 468 / 1 506 / 1 500 | +2.2 / +4.9 / +4.5 % |
| **forged** (real sender, newer sequence, another key's signature), no policy | **1 078** | refused at the signature |
| forged, through cheap checks / with a member's tag (insider) | 1 110 / 1 145 | |
| forged, refused by the rate cap | 61 | |
| **outsider's frame, refused by the tag** | **22.5** | the check alone: 17.0 |
| garbage / replay / unknown sender, refused by cheap checks | 22.6 / 30.6 / 30.2 | |

A forged frame costs a quarter less than an honest one. An honest frame also pays for its
storage (contradiction check, store, frame index), about 360 µs that a forged frame never
reaches.

### Scoring

* Plan (1), about 700 forged frames/s saturate one core: **MISSED** — 928 /s (1/1.078 ms), a
  third more, because a forged frame is cheaper than an honest one. A loaded receiver fails
  sooner: 247 forged frames/s on top of 41 senders.
* Plan (2):
  * an outsider needs more than 100 000 frames/s to saturate a core: **MISSED** — 44 400 /s
    (1/22.5 µs).
  * "which the channel cannot carry": **HELD** — one 802.11a 6 Mb/s channel delivers at most
    2 857 such frames/s (0.35 ms each), 15 times fewer.
  * Outsider flooding effectively removed: **HELD** — V ≥ 0.984 at 3 000 /s.
* Plan (3):
  * an insider remains a threat: **HELD** (129 /s).
  * The cap limits the damage to the impersonated sender: **HELD for one named sender up to
    2 000 /s.** The other 40 senders' V is then between 0.960 and 0.985 (bounds from the total).
    At 3 000 /s the refusals alone, 61 µs each, fill the core.
  * **NOT HELD when the insider names every sender.** Spread thin, the forgeries fit under each
    sender's cap: none was refused for rate at 250 /s. At 2 000 /s and above the caps refuse
    honest frames as well as forged ones. The cap is no better than no defence (V < 0.95 from
    123 /s, against 247 /s).
* Plan (4), about 2 % of N_max by Eq. (6): **NOT HELD as stated.** Eq. (6) gives 2.6 % for an
  8 B tag per frame, computed before registration.
* A7-1:
  * a forged frame within 10 % of an honest one: **MISSED** (−25 %);
  * ≈ 1.4 ms: **MISSED** (1.08 ms);
  * ≈ 700 /s per core: **MISSED** (928).
* A7-2:
  * the tag check under 20 µs: **HELD** (17.0; 22.5 with the refusal);
  * more than 50 000 frames/s per core: **MISSED** (44 400);
  * more than the channel carries and "effectively removed": **HELD**.
* A7-3, the tag costs 3 % of capacity by the no-fit closed form: **HELD** (3.1 %, arithmetic).
* A7-4:
  * cheap checks stop nothing of the worst case: **HELD** (no better than none in the flood);
  * they stop replays and garbage at about 0.1 ms: **MISSED on the cost** (0.023–0.031 ms, four
    times cheaper).

**The batch row (arithmetic).** With Ed25519 verified in batches of 64 at 0.46 of a single
verification, an honest frame through cheap checks would cost 1 326 µs instead of 1 468: 10 %
less, because the signature is only 18 % of the Python receiver's work. The compiled receiver
(A8) is where batching pays.

**What it means.**

* **Outsiders:** the 8 B group tag is the defence that works. An outsider's frame costs a
  receiver 22.5 µs, and the channel cannot carry enough of them to matter.
* **Insiders:** cheap checks do nothing against a well-formed forgery. A per-sender cap
  protects only against an impostor of one sender. An insider holding the group key, spreading
  forgeries over every sender, breaks the Python receiver at about 130 forged frames/s, which
  is 5 % of what the channel carries.
* **The compiled receiver (arithmetic from A8's costs):** an unprotected Rust receiver with 124
  senders would fail near 2 000 forged frames/s, still within the channel's 2 857. The tag is
  needed with either receiver.
* **What the tag cannot do:** stop a member. Neither the tag nor the cap removes the insider.
  That stays a stated limitation.
