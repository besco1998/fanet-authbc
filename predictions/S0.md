## What the driver source says, read before any data (Law 2)

* **Present per frame:** the dBm signal (`phy_info.recv_signal_power`), the rate (CCK below
  index 4), the channel, the flags. The FCS is appended to every frame.
* **TSFT:** emitted only if `pattrib->tsfl` is non-zero, and `af3bf004` never sets it.
  * The chip's receive descriptor carries the TSF's low word at dword 5, which mainline `rtl8xxxu`
    uses as the frame's start time (`RX_FLAG_MACTIME_START`).
  * The approved build copies it, so TSFT is expected, in µs, as 32 bits that wrap every 71.6 min
    (unwrapped by `s0.unwrap_tsft`).
* **Bad-checksum frames are dropped** in monitor mode. `RCR_ACRC32` is left off, and
  `usb_ops_linux.c` skips `crc_err` frames outside MP mode. So collisions cannot be seen directly.
* **The short-preamble flag is never set** (`if (0)`), so the preamble cannot be read here. C1 will
  infer it from the airtime intercept.
* **USB receive aggregation cannot be turned off,** so host times are bunched (about 0.5 ms). The
  timing must come from TSFT.
* **The WL18xx:** wlcore 5.10 lists no monitor mode, and "the fw doesn't provide an api to
  configure the filters … based on the active roles".

## The session (`experiments/channel24/plan_s0.txt`, 15 windows, about 8 minutes)

* **The layout:** W1s's 1 m triangle as it stands (L2), the TP-Link dongle beside the Pi 3, 1 m
  from pi-A, recorded in `geometry.json` (`analysis/geometry.py` must say "complete").
* **Channel 11,** frame burst off and read back, the redrawn sender, every power read back.
* **The windows:**
  * rate checks R1–R3 at 600 frames/s;
  * G1–G3: each Pi alone at 50 frames/s, 145 B, 23 s, so 1 000 frames sent in 20 s, at 15 dBm;
  * P15, P12, P9, P6: pi-A alone at 50 frames/s at 15, 12, 9 and 6 dBm, 25 s each;
  * X: the saturated pi-A + pi-B pair, 25 s;
  * **W:** pi-A at 50 frames/s, 60 s, while Blue 1 runs the WL18xx retest (an AP role on channel
    11 with a monitor interface beside it, untried in F121);
  * R1–R3 again.
* **The sniffer** records from 60 s before the first window to 60 s after the last. Those two
  minutes, with the Pis in their cell but silent, are the channel-occupancy captures (reported).

## Pass criteria (scored; all required)

| criterion | measured as | passes if |
|---|---|---|
| **capture completeness** | in G1, the share of pi-A's sequence numbers 0 … sent − 1 that the sniffer recorded | **≥ 0.99** (G2 and G3 reported) |
| **TSFT** | the share of our frames carrying TSFT | **≥ 0.99** |
| **TSFT is per frame, in µs** | in each saturated rate check, consecutive sequence numbers' TSFT differences | **< 1 %** below the airtime (1 864 µs), **and** the median within **2 %** of the receivers' cycle |
| **signal follows set power** | pi-A's mean dBm at 12, 9 and 6 dBm, less its mean at 15 | each within **±1.5 dB** of −3, −6, −9 |
| **rate** | our frames decoded at 1 Mb/s | **every one** |

**Reported, not scored:**
* the preamble ("not readable");
* bad-checksum frames in X (expected "no");
* G2 and G3's completeness;
* the mean signal per setting;
* the WL18xx retest's frames of pi-A (expected 0);
* the silent minutes' other transmitters and busy fraction;
* without TSFT, the host time's jitter against pi-B (W1p's robust form).

**If any scored criterion fails, the gate fails:** stop, report which, and ask Mohamed. The
AR9271 purchase is the fallback.

### Seen after scoring — not a result

* **The TSFT failure is the registration's statistic, not the timestamps.**
  * The median of a skewed interval distribution was compared with the receivers' mean cycle.
    W1s had shown 37–46 % of gaps at DIFS, so the median was bound to fall below the mean.
  * The sniffer's **mean** start-to-start equals the receivers' cycle within 0.03–0.25 % in all six
    windows (2 047.5 against 2 049.8 µs, …).
  * A corrected gate (S0b) will compare mean with mean, on new windows.
* **The completeness failure is the adapter's.**
  * The window spans were not the cause. The sniffer's clock ran 18–43 ms from the Pis', inside the
    ±0.5 s margin.
  * The missing frames are scattered: 110 of pi-A's 1 000 in 99 separate runs.
  * The Pis received the same frames at 99.8–100 %.
  * **D1a** (same positions, 19:36, disclosed), at 145 / 40 / 1 000 B:
    * pi-A 0.971 / 0.974 / 0.947;
    * pi-B 0.989 / 0.986 / 0.988;
    * the Pi 3 0.982.
  * **D1b** (pi-A and pi-B swapped in place, 19:50): pi-A 0.948, pi-B 0.952, the Pi 3 0.970, the
    Pi 3 unmoved.
  * So the misses **follow neither the board nor its place, and drift between sessions**
    (pi-A 0.89 → 0.971, the Pi 3 0.987 → 0.970).
  * They are not more frequent at strong signal: pi-A at 6 dBm (−47.7 dBm) was missed 5.4 %, at
    15 dBm 4.8 %; in D1b, at −49 dBm, 5.2 %.
  * They are not tcpdump drops (0 in all three captures), and the driver logged no checksum skips:
    with checksum-error acceptance off, the chip discards bad frames in hardware.
* **The compression of the signal steps: most likely the transmitter, not established.** In the
  same windows the Pi 3's firmware reading fell 0, 3 and 7 dB, so two receivers of different chips
  both register about 0.7 dB per dB of pi-A's set power. A receiver-side cause would need both to
  compress alike. No reference (an attenuator) is in the kit.
