# A16 — a post-mission ledger audit and merge

*Registered 2026-10-20 before any run of `analysis/ledger_audit.py`. Plan: `docs/AUTHBC Revision
Plan.md`, A16. Outcome appended below a line at the end.*

## What is built and run

`src/authbc/ledger/audit.py`: each UAV uploads its own frames and the neighbours' frames it
kept; the audit checks each sender's own chain (frames verify alone, contiguous sequence, every
link the hash of the record before), checks every held frame against the sender's own chain,
collects equivocation proofs and forgeries, and publishes a Merkle root over every audited
frame, signed by the auditor. Unit tests: `tests/unit/ledger/test_audit.py`.

`analysis/ledger_audit.py` → `results/raw/ledger_audit.csv`: three UAVs, **one hour**, 50
records/s, four records a frame (45 000 frames each), independent loss 5 % on every link, the
real sender and receiver. Planted: UAV 2 equivocates on its frame 10 000 (a second version to
UAV 3); UAV 3 adds a frame in UAV 1's name, signed with its own key, at a number it lost.
The audit is timed over the whole hour — a measured figure per flight hour, not a scaled one.

## Computed before this registration — arithmetic

* A receiver keeps **frames** (`LeanReceiver._frames`; a record is evidence only with its
  frame): 172.999 B per four records = **43.25 B per record**, **7.79 MB per sender-hour**,
  **0.97 GB per hour for 124 neighbours**.
* A decoded record in canonical form is **134 B** (the generator's records; 133–135 B), i.e.
  24.1 MB per sender-hour, 3.0 GB per hour for 124 neighbours.
* The plan's "about 60 B per ledger record" is neither: the kept frame is smaller, the decoded
  record larger.

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> both injected faults are detected. Storage is the likely surprise: 50 records/s for an hour
> at about 60 B per ledger record is about 11 MB per sender, so a node storing 124 neighbors
> needs about 1.3 GB per hour. Report this and propose storing neighbors at a reduced rate.

Scored as: (1) `equivocation_detected` = 1 and `forgery_detected` = 1; (2) storage per
sender-hour as measured, against 11 MB and 1.3 GB for 124 neighbours.

## Mine `[agent]`

* **A16-1.** Both faults detected and **no other finding** (`false_findings` = 0): no gap, broken
  link or mismatch in an honest chain. (The equivocation also makes UAV 3 refuse UAV 2's next
  frame as TAMPERED — its link no longer matches what UAV 3 holds; that is the receiver
  working, not an audit finding.)
* **A16-2.** Kept storage measured at 43.25 B per record, 7.79 MB per sender-hour, within 0.1 %
  of the arithmetic above; the plan's 11 MB / 1.3 GB is **not** what the design stores.
* **A16-3.** About 391 000 frames are audited (135 000 own, ≈ 256 500 held, one planted). The
  audit costs 7–10 minutes of one core per flight hour of three UAVs (one decode and one Ed25519
  verification per frame in the Python prototype, ≈ 1.1–1.5 ms each on this host).

## What this cannot show

Ledgers made over a synthetic independent loss, not ns-3 traces or flights; three UAVs; an
auditor that is trusted. The audit catches what one valid signature proves — it cannot recover
a frame no one kept.

## Outcome (2026-10-20; one flight hour, three UAVs)

`results/raw/ledger_audit.csv`. The first run of this script died in the out-of-memory crash
before it wrote anything; the run below is the second, unchanged.

| | result |
|---|---|
| Plan (1): both faults detected | **HELD** — the equivocation (UAV 2, frame 10 000) and the planted forgery |
| A16-1: no other finding | **HELD** — `false_findings` = 0 over 391 495 frames |
| Plan (2): ~11 MB per sender-hour, ~1.3 GB per hour for 124 neighbours | **NOT what is stored:** frames kept **7.73 MB** per sender-hour (42.94 B a record) → **0.96 GB** per hour for 124; decoded records would be 23.8 MB (132.0 B a record) → 2.9 GB |
| A16-2: kept bytes within 0.1 % of 43.25 B a record | **FAILED: 42.94 (−0.7 %).** Cause, checked: the arithmetic used the sizing convention (sender id 40 000, sequence numbers an hour into a flight); the mission uses sender ids 1–3 and sequences from 0, which CBOR writes shorter — the same frames cost 42.18 B a record at the start of the hour and 42.75 B by sequence 100 000 |
| A16-3: 7–10 min of one core per flight hour | **FAILED (faster): 6.4 min** (386 s, 1 014 frames a second, 0.99 ms a frame) |

**What it means.** The ledger now has the step that makes it auditable: one pass over the
uploads proves who equivocated and who planted a forgery, with nothing but the public keys, and
publishes a signed Merkle root per mission. It costs 6.4 minutes of one core per flight hour of
three UAVs in the Python prototype, linear in frames. Keeping frames, not decoded records, is
also the cheaper store — about 1 GB per hour for 124 neighbours; storing neighbours at a
reduced rate (the plan's proposal) would cut that in proportion.
