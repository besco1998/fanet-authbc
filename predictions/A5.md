# A5 — a time window against frames an adversary holds back

*Registered 2026-10-20, before any run of `analysis/replay_window.py`. Plan: `docs/AUTHBC
Revision Plan.md`, A5. Never edited after the first run; outcomes are appended below a line at
the end.*

## Why

The receiver refuses a replay by sequence number only. A frame it has not seen is accepted
however old it is, so a relay that delays everything it forwards is never detected. The paper
lists delayed replay as not protected.

## The rule (`src/authbc/placement/freshness_rule.py`, committed in `d4bf273`)

    t_local − ts_newest ≤ Δ_acc = b/Λ + T_ch + t_v + 2σ_clk + g

at zero extra bytes: `ts` is already in every record and signed with it. It is a layer around
`LeanReceiver` (the timed, hashed `session_v2.py` is not edited) and checks a frame's own
timestamp before its signature: a timestamp altered to look fresh then fails the signature
(held by `tests/unit/placement/test_freshness_rule.py`). In a GNSS outage the window widens by
the worst drift of both clocks; past a limit the rule stands aside and marks what it accepts
TIME_UNVERIFIED.

## What is run

`analysis/replay_window.py` → `results/raw/replay_window.csv`. Real lean frames (b = 4,
50 records/s, a keyframe in every frame), 30 seeds × 2 400 frames per cell.

* **Clocks.** Each timestamp and each local reading carries an independent Gaussian error of
  σ ∈ {1, 10, 50} ms, so their difference has σ√2. In an outage of t, both clocks drift by up to
  20 ppm, in the worst case in opposite directions: 2·20·10⁻⁶·t apart = **144 ms per hour**.
* **Honest timing.** A frame leaves when its last record is taken and is verified
  T = 3.7502 ms later (worst channel delay + one verification, as Eq. 2).
* **Adversary: a delay line.** From a frame drawn uniformly in the first half of the stream to
  the end, every frame arrives δ late, δ ∈ {10, 50, 100, 250, 1 000, 10 000} ms. (A frame
  delayed alone is already refused by sequence number once a later one has arrived; the delay
  line is the attack sequence numbers cannot see.)
* **Policies.** `seq-only` (the published receiver); `fixed150` (Δ_acc = 150 ms, the plan's
  number); `formula` (Δ_acc = 80 + 3.75 + 2σ + g, g = 10 ms: 95.75, 113.75, 193.75 ms).
* **Outages** of 0–240 min in 15 min steps at σ = 10 ms: fixed windows of 150 and 250 ms, and
  the formula window widened by the outage the receiver knows of.

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> - With σ ≤ 10 ms and Δ_acc = 150 ms, honest false rejection stays below 10⁻⁴, and every delay
>   above 150 ms is detected.
> - Two clocks drifting at 20 ppm in opposite directions separate by 72 ms per hour, so a
>   250 ms window survives about 2 hours of outage.

Scored as: (1) `fixed150`, σ ∈ {1, 10}: false rejection < 10⁻⁴ and detection = 1.000 at every
δ ∈ {250, 1 000, 10 000}; (2) `fixed250` under outage: the longest outage on the 15 min grid
with false rejection < 10⁻⁴ lies within 90–150 min ("about 2 hours", ±30 min).

## Predictions — mine `[agent]`

* **A5-0 (arithmetic, disputed before running).** Two clocks at 20 ppm in opposite directions
  separate by **144 ms per hour**, not 72 (72 ms is one clock). Held by a unit test.
* **A5-1 (sequence numbers alone).** `seq-only` accepts every delayed frame at every δ:
  detection 0.000.
* **A5-2 (the window, Gaussian tails computed beforehand).** `fixed150`: false rejection 0 at
  σ = 1 and 10, **≈ 0.019** at σ = 50; detection ≈ 0 for δ ≤ 100 at σ ≤ 10 (those delays are
  inside the window by construction: the rule bounds an adversary's delay to about
  Δ_acc − T = 146 ms, it does not remove it), 1.000 for δ ≥ 250 at σ ≤ 10, and ≈ 0.93 at
  δ = 250 with σ = 50. `formula`: false rejection 0 at σ ≤ 10, ≈ 0.004 at σ = 50 (2σ covers a
  difference whose spread is σ√2 only to 1.4 of its own deviations); at σ = 10, detection at
  δ = 100 ≈ 0.24 and 1.000 for δ ≥ 250. Each within its binomial 95 % interval.
* **A5-3 (outage).** A fixed 250 ms window at σ = 10 ms keeps false rejection below 10⁻⁴ up to
  **75 min** on the grid (the tail passes 10⁻⁴ near 81 min) and is above 10⁻² at 90 min; a fixed
  150 ms window up to 30 min. So the plan's "about 2 hours" is expected to **fail** as scored
  above; it would hold only if one clock alone drifted (72 ms/h: ≈ 2.7 h). The widening window
  keeps false rejection at its no-outage value at every outage of the grid; the price is that
  the delay an adversary can add grows by 144 ms per hour. Arithmetic, not run: with a limit of
  250 ms the formula window (113.75 ms at σ = 10) gives way after 0.95 h, with 1 s after 6.2 h.

## Disclosure

No run of `replay_window.py` exists. The rule's unit tests ran on hand-built timestamps
(`test_freshness_rule.py`). The window values above were printed from the script's functions
before this commit; the tails are Gaussian arithmetic.

## What this cannot show

Clock errors are modelled, not measured; the GNSS receiver's own time error and an adversary
that spoofs GNSS are out of scope (a spoofed clock moves the window itself). A delay line that
stays under the window is not detected — the rule bounds the delay, it does not forbid it.

**What it means.** At zero bytes the rule closes the delay-line attack the published receiver
cannot see, while GNSS time is held: every frame held back by 250 ms or more is refused, and an
adversary's added delay is bounded by the window less the honest delay (≈ 146 ms at Δ = 150).
It does not forbid delays inside the window. Its cost is clock quality: at σ = 50 ms a 150 ms
window refuses 2 % of honest frames, and in a GNSS outage a fixed window fails within about an
hour; a window widened by the worst drift keeps working, at the price of letting the bound grow
by 144 ms an hour.
