# A10 — TESLA, EMSS and Wong–Lam implemented, and verified against a deadline

*Registered 2026-10-20 before any run of `ns3/authbc-links.cc` with traces. Plan: `docs/AUTHBC
Revision Plan.md`, A10. Outcome appended below a line at the end.*

## What is built and run

* `src/authbc/placement/stream_schemes.py` — TESLA (one-way key chain, 10 B MAC, key disclosed
  d intervals later, the safe-packet test), EMSS (two digests per data packet, a signed packet
  of digests after every k), Wong–Lam (Merkle tree over blocks of four, the root signed with
  Ed25519). Unit tests: authentication at the moment each definition says, refusal of forgeries,
  the bytes Table 5 charged (EMSS adds a 1 B packet-type tag, an implementation detail; the
  simulated frames are Table 5's sizes).
* `ns3/authbc-links.cc` — the published scenario plus a trace of 20 sender→receiver pairs per
  run (absent by default; at its defaults the binary reproduced published runs bit for bit:
  cell C, N = 35, seeds 1 and 2, `results/raw/links_identity.csv`).
* `analysis/stream_deadline.py` — each scheme run on each trace; a record counts if verified
  within D_max = 100 ms of being taken (the timing rule is in the script's docstring). Grids:
  `experiments/stream-deadline/plan.txt`, 30 seeds. **Substitution, stated:** the schemes run on
  traces of frames of their own size and rate rather than as ns-3 applications; with broadcast
  and no feedback the two are the same (`docs/revision/EXECUTION_PLAN.md`). EMSS with k = 4, 8,
  16 is simulated as one frame of the data and signature packets' mean size at their joint rate.

Cells and the access-rule model's delivery crossing (computed before this commit): MAVLink 2
(94 B) 42.3; TESLA (106 B) 40.5; Gennaro–Rohatgi (114 B) 39.5; EMSS k = 100 (146 B, 50.5 /s)
34.8; Wong–Lam (211 B) 28.7; TESLA with four records a frame (133 B, 12.5 /s) 141.3; EMSS
k = 4, 8, 16 (159, 153, 150 B) 27.3, 30.8, 32.8.

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> - Delivery Nmax reproduces Table 5 within its CIs: 42, 40, 39, 34 and 28 nodes.
> - Deadline-aware V falls well below delivery for TESLA and EMSS whenever the key disclosure or
>   signature interval approaches Dmax. Their real capacity under the freshness constraint is
>   therefore below the Table 5 upper bound.
> - TESLA with b = 4 gives the smallest frames of all, but still without non-repudiation. This
>   supports the claim that the frame, not the authenticator, binds.

Scored as: (1) delivery N_max (first-failure grid crossing) of LSM, LST, LSG, LSE, LSW inside
Table 5's intervals ([41, 42], [40, 40], [39, 39], [34, 34], [28, 29]); (2) for TESLA with
d·I ≥ 50 ms and EMSS with k·20 ms ≥ 80 ms, the in-time crossing is below the delivery
crossing, or absent (V never reaches 0.95); (3) TESLA-batch4's frame is the smallest per record
of all cells (133/4 = 33.25 B against 43.25 for the design) and its delivery crossing the
highest.

## Mine `[agent]`

* **A10-1.** Each delivery crossing within ±3 % of the access-rule model's value above.
* **A10-2.** In-time V equals delivery exactly for MAVLink 2 and Wong–Lam (they verify on
  arrival; Wong–Lam's records wait 60 ms at the sender, inside 100 ms).
* **A10-3.** Gennaro–Rohatgi has no in-time crossing on any grid point: one lost packet ends
  authentication for the rest of a 20 s stream, so V_in_time ≈ (number of packets before the
  first loss) / 1 000 — near 0.
* **A10-4.** TESLA with I = 10 or 20 ms and d = 1 crosses within 10 % below its delivery
  crossing; with I = 50 ms and d = 2 it has no crossing (the key alone arrives 50–150 ms late).
* **A10-5.** EMSS with k = 100 and k = 16 have no in-time crossing; k = 4 crosses below its
  delivery crossing.
* **A10-6 (the batch is not a fix for TESLA).** TESLA with four records a frame delivers best of
  all (≈ 141 nodes) but its in-time V stays far below 0.95 at every grid point: its key comes
  one frame period (80 ms) after the frame, on top of up to 60 ms of fill.

* Plan (1), delivery N_max inside Table 5's intervals: **HELD** for all five (42, 40, 39, 34,
  28; Wong–Lam's 29 nodes deliver 0.94999).
* Plan (2), TESLA with d·I ≥ 50 ms and EMSS with k·20 ms ≥ 80 ms in time below delivery or
  absent: **HELD** (TESLA I = 50: 30.3 and none; EMSS k = 4, 8, 16, 100: none).
* Plan (3), TESLA-batch4 the smallest frame per record and the highest delivery crossing:
  **HELD** (33.25 B; 141.3). Without non-repudiation, and (A10-6) without verifying in time.
* A10-1, delivery within ±3 % of the model: **HELD** for all nine (largest +1.0 %, Wong–Lam).
* A10-2, in time equal to delivery for MAVLink 2 and Wong–Lam: **MISSED as worded** — in-time V
  is computed on the 20 traced pairs of a run, delivery on all pairs, and they differ by up to
  0.0006 either way; the crossings agree within 0.1 node.
* A10-3, Gennaro–Rohatgi near 0: **HELD** (0.018–0.021).
* A10-4: TESLA with I = 10 or 20 ms and d = 1 crossing within 10 % below delivery — **MISSED**
  (no crossing); I = 50, d = 2 with none — **HELD**. **Why d = 1 is worse than d = 2 at short
  intervals,** checked: a TESLA receiver must drop a packet that may arrive after its key is
  out (the safe-packet test). With d = 1 the key of interval i is out when interval i ends, and
  under the registered timing rule a frame arrives 1 ms after it is sent, so a share 1 ms / I of
  packets is unsafe: the measured in-time/delivery ratios are 0.8995, 0.949, 0.978 at I = 10,
  20, 50 against 0.90, 0.95, 0.98. ns-3's own frame delay is 0.43 ms on average, so the 1 ms
  rule is pessimistic for d = 1 by about half.
* A10-5: EMSS k = 100 and 16 without a crossing **HELD**; k = 4 crossing below delivery
  **MISSED** — 0.949 at 20 nodes, the lowest grid point, so no crossing on the grid.
* A10-6: **HELD** (in-time V ≈ 0.23 at every grid point).

**What it means.** Against a 100 ms deadline Table 5 changes as the plan expected, and more:
MAVLink 2 and Wong–Lam keep their capacities (42, 29); TESLA keeps 39.6–39.8 only with d = 2 and
I ≤ 20 ms (and has no non-repudiation); Gennaro–Rohatgi, every EMSS, and TESLA with records
batched in a frame verify too little in time to cross at any node count simulated. Footnote 2
("an upper bound") can be replaced by these simulated, deadline-aware columns.
