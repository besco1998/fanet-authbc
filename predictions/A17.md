# A17 — the accounting applied to ADS-B, Remote ID and V2X CAM

*Registered 2026-10-20 before `analysis/other_links.py` was run. Analysis only: every number
it produces is arithmetic on sourced sizes and rates and on the codec's emitted frames. Plan:
`docs/AUTHBC Revision Plan.md`, A17. Outcome appended below a line at the end.*

## What is computed

For each system, Eq. (5) (does a frame that verifies alone fit one transmission?), the largest
batch its payload admits, and Eq. (2) at deadlines of 100 ms to 5 s (b* with the paper's
T_ch + t_v). The frame is the lean design's, emitted at the system's message spacing (capped
at 1 s by the generator). Sizes and rates, each from a source held and read:

| system | payload per transmission | rate | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADS-B 1090ES | 56-bit ME field of a 112-bit message (7 B) | ≈ 2 /s | Strohmeier et al. 2015, p. 2 |
| Remote ID, Bluetooth 4 legacy | 25 octets | 1 /s | RFC 9575 §3.2.4.1, §6.2 |
| Remote ID, Bluetooth 5 / Wi-Fi | Message Pack of ≤ 9 × 25 = 225 octets | 1 /s | RFC 9575 §6.2 |
| V2X CAM | 802.11p, 1500 B MTU | 1–10 Hz | ETSI EN 302 637-2 V1.4.1 §6.1.3 |

**A deadline for CAM is not in its standard.** EN 302 637-2 bounds the generation interval
(100–1000 ms) and the time to generate one CAM (< 50 ms, §6.1.5.1), not the delivery. The
plan's "100 ms deadline" is therefore swept with the others, not quoted as the standard's.

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> ADS-B and Bluetooth 4 Remote ID cannot carry a self-verifying frame; Bluetooth 5 and Wi-Fi
> Remote ID can. CAM at 10 Hz with a 100 ms deadline has ΛDmax = 1, so no batch is admitted.
> That explains, from your own rule, why V2X standards sign each message.

## Mine `[agent]`

* **A17-1.** ADS-B (7 B) and Bluetooth 4 Remote ID (25 B) are excluded by the 64 B signature
  alone, in any format; the Message Pack (225 B) holds our one-record frame and a batch of a
  few records.
* **A17-2.** Stronger than the plan for CAM: under Eq. (2)'s whole-period convention a 10 Hz
  stream with a 100 ms deadline admits **no** frame at all (100 + 3.75 ms > 100 ms), and a batch
  of two needs a deadline of at least 203.75 ms. At 1 Hz a batch needs more than 2 s.
* **A17-3 (wording).** The rule shows that batching buys nothing at CAM's rates under deadlines
  of 200 ms or less. That is *consistent with* per-message signing; it does not show why the
  standards chose it (EN 302 637-2 gives no reason). The text will say "consistent with".
