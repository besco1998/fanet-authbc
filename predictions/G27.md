# G27 — which rule for a radio's own queued frames fits all three hardware measurements?

*Registered 2026-11-03, before any candidate rule was simulated with two stations. Open item G27
(`docs/OPEN_ITEMS.md` §4, register G), started on Mohamed's word "do g27". Code at this commit:
`src/authbc/sim/dcf_own_queue.py`, `analysis/own_queue_rules.py`.*

## Why

Three measurements on two Raspberry Pi 4 radios (BCM4345/6), all of 2026-10-09:

| | measured | the standard's rule |
|---|---|---|
| **F74, F75:** one saturated sender, gap after its 1976 µs frame | **44.8 µs** with frame burst on (mean of 4 windows, 42.6–50.5); **73.8 µs** off (2 windows) | 101.5 µs |
| **F76:** two saturated senders, frames on air lost | 12.14 % on, 12.40 % off | 11.8 % |
| **F76:** two saturated senders, total frames on air per second | **529.6** on, 525.3 off | 520 |
| **F73:** two boards below saturation, loss at occupancy 0.50 / 0.70 / 0.85 | on: 0.334 / 0.498 / 0.749 %; off: 0.321 / 0.516 / 0.837 % | model 0.29 / 0.64 / **1.40 %** |

**A flaw in F76's reading.** F76's registration assumed that the way a radio sends its own queued
frames "never changes what happens when two radios contend frame by frame". In saturation,
though, every station always holds a queue, so an own-queue rule acts at every frame there too.
F76 therefore constrains the rule; it does not separate the rule from the contention. The rate
on air with frame burst on, 529.6 against 520, already shows shorter gaps under contention.

So the question is: **is there a rule for the radio's own queued frames that gives the
one-sender spacing, keeps two saturated senders where they were measured, and gives the 36
windows?** If there is, F73's shortfall at high load is *consistent with* it. If none of the
rules tried passes, the reading of F76 that the paper and thesis rest on loses its support.

## The data, and the role each plays

| role | file(s) | what it may do |
|---|---|---|
| **calibration** | `results/hw/channel/frame_spacing.csv` (6 one-sender windows) | sets each rule's one parameter per state |
| **screen (stage B)** | `saturated_2nodes_fb_{on,off}.csv` (2 × 4 windows, 16 rows) | removes a rule; never sets a parameter |
| **test (stage C)** | `contention_2nodes{,_fb_off,_fb_on_repeat}_windows.csv` (36 windows) | scores a rule; never sets a parameter |

⚠️ **This is a postdiction.** Every number above has been published since 2026-10-09 and I have
seen all of them, including F73's per-state means. The protection against fitting is
procedural:

* the rules come from sources;
* each rule has one parameter per state, fixed by a closed form from the calibration data alone;
* the code is committed and pushed before any rule is simulated with two stations;
* nothing is adjusted afterwards, and every rule is reported, the losers included.

The real prediction is G27's second half: a surviving rule run on hardware at loads not yet
measured. That half needs the boards and is Mohamed's to decide.

## Found while preparing it: a defect of the frozen model (F114)

The new module copies the event loop of `src/authbc/sim/dcf_unsaturated.py`, which is frozen.
Its first test failed. One simulated saturated sender spaced its frames by 94.6 µs where the
standard's arithmetic gives 101.5 µs, and by 27.9 µs, **less than DIFS**, when every counter was
set to 0.

**Cause.** A frame that reaches a station inside the detection window of that station's own
frame made the station a second sender of the same group. The station collided with itself:
two frames were counted and both counted lost.

**The fix.** In `dcf_own_queue` the frame waits in its station's queue. Everywhere the frozen
model forms no such group, the two agree bit for bit (`tests/test_dcf_own_queue.py`).

**How much it mattered.** Measured from the frozen model's own groups, 30 seeds per point
(`results/raw/g27_frozen_defect.csv`):

| where the frozen model is used | loss, frozen → without the defect |
|---|---|
| the capacity cells (C at N = 28 and 35, D at N = 97 and 124) | unchanged (1–2 frames in about 900 000) |
| F73's points, two boards at 124 / 174 / 211 frames/s | −1.0 % / −2.0 % / −1.7 % of the loss |
| F76's model figure | 11.90 % → 11.71 % (2/17 = 11.76 %) |
| the registered grid, three to five boards | −0.0 to −0.5 % of the loss |

No registered verdict and no capacity moves. The frozen file is not edited: its registration
holds it byte for byte.

**A second convention, examined and kept.** An arrival during an idle period moves the slot grid
to the arrival, so a running counter loses the part of a slot that had gone by. This adds
+0.2–0.6 µs to one saturated sender's gap at 500–2000 arrivals per second. That is the size the
convention predicts, and it applies to every rule alike.

## The rules

Constants: PPDU 1976 µs, DIFS 34 µs, slot 9 µs, SIFS 16 µs, W0 16, detection time 4 µs. Each rule
is an `OwnQueueRule`; `candidates()` in the analysis sets its parameter from the measured gap g.

| | rule | source | one sender's mean gap | on (g = 44.8 µs) | off (g = 73.8 µs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R0 | the standard (control) | docs/02 §6g | DIFS + 7.5 slots = 101.5 µs | — | — |
| R1 | every counter drawn from a smaller window W | Bianchi et al., INFOCOM 2007: Ralink and Realtek 7, Broadcom under Linux 15, where 31 is required | DIFS + (W−1)/2 slots | W = 3 | W = 10 |
| R2 | the post-transmission counter is 0 with probability q | ibid., the Prism II card | DIFS + (1−q)·67.5 µs | q = 0.84 | q = 0.41 |
| R3 | burst: with probability q the next queued frame is sent SIFS after the station's own frame, without contending | the frame-burst mode the driver switches on; the standard's TXOP | q·SIFS + (1−q)·101.5 µs | q = 0.663 | q = 0.324 |
| R4 | the smaller window only after the station's own frame; a counter interrupted by another station's frame is drawn again from W0 | ibid. §IV-B: cards that misbehave alone "now behave properly" after a busy period | as R1 | W = 3 | W = 10 |
| R5 | a shorter backoff slot σ, DIFS kept | **no source**: a timing family, disclosed as such | DIFS + 7.5 σ | σ = 1.44 µs | σ = 5.31 µs |

The windows of R1 and R4 are whole numbers, so their gaps are 43.0 and 74.5 µs, within half a slot
of the measured gaps.

**Considered and not run.** Counting the post-transmission counter from the end of the frame
instead of from DIFS after it. It has no source and no parameter. I noticed it by arithmetic on
the "off" spacing (it gives 72.6 µs), and it cannot give the "on" spacing.

**Sensitivity, reported and never used to change a verdict.** Each rule is also set from:

* the lowest single window in each state;
* the highest single window in each state;
* **reading B**, in which the frames the receiver missed never went on air (on 61.5 µs, off
  84.4 µs).

All the values are in `results/raw/g27_calibration.csv`.

## Stage A — done before this registration (a test of the code, not of the rules)

One saturated sender, offered 900 frames/s, 10 seeds × 20 s. For all 48 sets (2 states × 4
calibrations × 6 rules) the simulated gap equals the closed form within 1 µs
(`results/raw/g27_calibration.csv`).

## Stage B — the screen against F76

**Method.** Two stations, each offered 400 frames/s, 30 seeds × 20 s. Only frames that start in
[1, 20) s are counted, while both queues are full. The quantities are the mean loss and the mean
total frames per second.

**The bands** (`saturated_bands()`). The measured mean ± its 95 % t-interval: loss over 8 rows,
rate over 4 windows. Each band reaches lower by what one saturated sender loses with nobody to
collide with: at most **0.919 pp** (`frame_spacing.csv`). That loss is not in the model, and a
frame that never reached the air would still be counted on air.

| state | loss band | rate band (frames/s) |
|---|---|---|
| on | 10.87–12.49 % | 522.0–532.3 |
| off | 10.90–12.98 % | 513.4–532.5 |

**A rule passes stage B in a state if its loss and its rate both lie inside that state's band.**

## Stage C — F73's 36 windows

**Method.** Two nodes at 124, 174 and 211 frames/s, each frame at an instant drawn anew in its
period, 30 seeds × 20 s. This is the setting of the registered predictions, plus their link loss
of 2.3·10⁻⁴.

**The bands** (`window_bands()`): the measured mean ± its 95 % t-interval over the windows of that
state.

| state (windows) | 124 frames/s | 174 frames/s | 211 frames/s |
|---|---|---|---|
| on (8) | 0.269–0.400 % | 0.417–0.579 % | 0.557–0.940 % |
| off (4) | 0.208–0.434 % | 0.306–0.726 % | 0.590–1.085 % |
| pooled (12; reported, not scored) | 0.284–0.376 % | 0.438–0.570 % | 0.648–0.908 % |

**A rule passes stage C in a state if it lies inside the band at all three loads.** Stage C is
run and reported for every rule.

**A rule is a survivor in a state only if, with the primary calibration (the mean gap), it passes
both stage B and stage C in that state.**

## The control, run before this registration

After the defect was fixed, R0 was run in stages B and C. It is not a candidate, and its outcome
was nearly known from the frozen model:

* **Stage B:** loss 11.80 % (2/17 = 11.76 %); rate 519.4 frames/s. It **passes "off" and fails
  "on"** on the rate (522.0 is the band's lower edge).
* **Stage C:** 0.290 / 0.624 / 1.372 %. It fails "on" at 174 and 211 frames/s, and "off" at 211.

## My expectations `[agent]` — low confidence, scored as stated

* **G27-1.** R1 fails stage B in both states, with loss above the band: about 2/(W+1), which is
  50 % on and 18 % off.
* **G27-2.** R2 and R3 fail stage B in both states, with loss below the band, because there are
  fewer contention rounds per frame.
* **G27-3.** R5 passes stage B "off" and fails it "on", with loss above the band: a slot of
  1.44 µs is shorter than the 4 µs detection time.
* **G27-4.** For R4 I have no confident expectation. I lean to it failing stage B "on".
* **G27-5.** No rule survives in both states.

## What each outcome means

| outcome | meaning | next |
|---|---|---|
| a survivor in a state | a rule consistent with all three measurements in that state: a postdiction, not a cause shown | G27-hw, a new registration with its predictions at loads not yet run; the session is Mohamed's |
| survivors of stage B, none of stage C | own-queue rules of these families do not account for F73's shortfall | the cause stays open; the paper's "the cause is not established" stands |
| no rule passes stage B | no tested own-queue rule that gives the one-sender spacing also gives two saturated senders | F76's reading "the shortfall comes from how a radio sends its own queued frames" loses the support of the saturated runs; a correction note goes in F76, and thesis wording is proposed to Mohamed |

## What this cannot show

* It is a postdiction (above).
* **Only the mean gap is measured; no single frame was timed.** Rules with the same mean give the
  same calibration and differ in the distribution of the gap, which nothing measured here can
  see.
* **The families are not exhaustive.** A rule outside them could pass where these fail.
* The model has no capture and no loss other than collisions. The link loss is the one measured
  at 100 frames/s.
* One chip family, two boards, one room.
