# A1 — a swarm spread out, faded, with hidden stations and motion

*Registered 2026-10-19, before any run of the scenarios below (d_ref, measured first as the
plan says, is in `predictions/A1-dref.md` with its outcome). Plan: `docs/AUTHBC Revision
Plan.md`, A1; open item G35. Never edited after the first run of stage 1; stage-2 grids are
committed separately before stage 2 (`predictions/A1-stage2.md`); outcomes are appended below
a line at the end.*

## Why

Every capacity of the paper is of one collision domain at equal received power, static, with
no hidden station. F85 relaxed equal power inside a 100 m disc (capacities rose by about an
eighth); hidden stations were left open as G35, the weak point that remains.

## The scenario (`ns3/authbc-spatial.cc`, committed in `b2b6ae6`)

The published traffic source, MAC and PHY rate, unchanged (802.11a, 6 Mb/s, ad-hoc broadcast,
send instant redrawn uniformly in each period, 20 s, seeds = RNG runs 1–30). The channel, as the
plan specifies: log-distance exponent 2 with 46.7 dB at 1 m, Nakagami m = 3 on every link,
20 dBm, `SimpleFrameCaptureModel` with a 4 dB margin; ns-3.48 defaults otherwise (preamble
detection 4 dB and −82 dBm). Three configurations: `base` = row 5 (146 B, 50 frames/s),
`inline4` = row 6 (372 B, 12.5 /s), `design` = row 8 (173 B, 12.5 /s).

**Measured first: d_ref = 215 m** (`results/raw/spatial_dref.csv`; expected 215–225 m). A node
r is a neighbour of s for a frame if they are within 215 m when it is sent or received.
**V of a receiver** = share of its neighbours' frames it received; the capacity is read from
the 30-seed mean over receivers of that share (the plan's "per-receiver V").

| scenario | layout | what it isolates |
|---|---|---|
| `fixed` | every node at one point, `FixedRssLossModel` −60 dBm, same code path | must reproduce the published crossings |
| `s1-r100`, `-r300`, `-r600`, `-r1000` | uniform in a disc of that radius, altitude 50–150 m | spread: neighbours ≪ contenders at large R |
| `s2` | three clusters of 20 m radius at 100 m altitude: A at −215 m, B at +215 m, M at 0 (separation 2·d_ref = 430 m) | A and B miss each other's preamble about a quarter of the time while M hears both: hidden stations among neighbours |
| `s3-v0`, `-v5`, `-v15` | group mobility: Gauss–Markov reference at 0 / 5 / 15 m/s in a 2 km box, members random-walking (2–4 m/s; still at speed 0) within ±60 m and ±50 m altitude | motion |

**Why S2 differs from the plan's wording.** The plan asks for "two clusters whose centers are
farther apart than the carrier-sense range but whose receivers sit between them". With this
channel a link delivers 99 % only within 215 m while a preamble is sensed, on average, to
582 m: two clusters farther apart than 582 m have no common neighbour within 215 m. At 2·d_ref
the clusters A and B sense each other with probability ≈ 0.75 per frame (Nakagami-3 at 430 m),
so hidden stations occur among genuine neighbours of M. Stated here as a substitution.

## Procedure — two stages, because of cost

One design run at R = 1000 m and N = 400 takes over ten minutes on this host; the plan's sweep
(N = 20…400 in steps of 20, 30 seeds, every scenario) would take about 300 CPU-hours.

* **Stage 1 — exploratory, seeds 1–5.** Grids: `base` N = 20, 40, 60, …; `inline4` and `design`
  N = 40, 80, 120, …; S2 in multiples of 3 (30, 60, … and 60, 120, …). Each grid is extended
  until two consecutive points have a 5-seed mean V below 0.93, or N = 800 (then the crossing
  is reported as censored). Stage 1 places the grids of stage 2 and is never reported as a
  capacity. S3 stage 1 runs at speed 0 only.
* **Stage 2 — scored, seeds 1–30.** Five node counts round(x₁(1 + 0.04k)), k = −2…2, x₁ the
  stage-1 interpolated crossing; if the 30-seed means do not bracket 0.95, points are added in
  4 % steps outward until they do, and each addition is reported. S3 at 5 and 15 m/s runs the
  speed-0 stage-2 grid. `fixed`: the published grids (C 32–38; D 115–133 in steps of 3;
  `inline4` 71–83 in steps of 2), no stage 1. Grids are committed (`predictions/A1-stage2.md`)
  before any stage-2 run.
* **Analysis:** `analysis/spatial_capacity.py --score` — interpolated crossing with the
  bootstrap interval of the paper's method, first-failure grid crossing, mean neighbours and
  density at the crossing, ratio to `base` with the interval from the two intervals' ends, and
  the hidden-station share defined in the script's docstring (fading-aware: the share of the
  nodes able to spoil a frame at r that cannot hear s's preamble).

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> - The fixed-RSS run reproduces Table 3 within its CIs (35 and 124).
> - In S1 at R = 1000 m and S2, absolute capacity falls below the single-domain value, because
>   hidden terminals add collisions.
> - **The ratio stays between 3.0× and 4.0×.** Reason: a hidden-node collision needs two
>   overlapping frames, and the design sends a quarter as many frames, each only about 15%
>   longer on air.
> - Mobility up to 15 m/s changes V by less than 0.01. A node moves at most 1.2 m during one
>   80 ms fill time.
> - If the ratio falls below 2.5×, report it as a finding and explain it with the hidden-node
>   share.

Scored as: (1) `fixed` N_max of C ∈ [35, 35] and of D ∈ [124, 125] (Table 3's intervals);
(2) at R = 1000 m and in S2 the design's and the baseline's capacity, **counted in neighbours**
(the plan's capacity), below the published 124.55 and 35.26; (3) design/base ratio of the
interpolated crossings ∈ [3.0, 4.0] in every scenario (inline4/base is reported, not scored);
(4) at every stage-2 node count of S3, |V(5 m/s) − V(0)| and |V(15 m/s) − V(0)| < 0.01 for each
configuration; (5) a reporting rule, not a prediction.

## Predictions — mine, added before any run `[agent]`

* **A1-1 (the code path).** `fixed` interpolated crossings within ±1 % of the published
  35.26 and 124.55 (published bootstrap intervals [35.15, 35.36] and [124.07, 125.02]).
  The plan's criterion (1) can fail on sampling alone with the right code — N_max is a grid
  point and the published interval of C is a single node — so this one is the stricter test of
  the code path and (1) is scored as well.
* **A1-2 (where I expect the effect to come from).** Spreading the swarm lowers the capacity
  counted in neighbours chiefly because a receiver contends with many more nodes than it

  - at R = 100 m (every pair within 215 m) and in S3 at speed 0, the crossings counted in

    [35.3, 46], design in [124.6, 165];
  - at R = 1000 m the design's crossing counted in **neighbours** lies in [10, 40];
  - the hidden-station share is below 0.02 at R = 100 m and between 0.03 and 0.20 at R = 1000 m,
    and it increases with R.
* **A1-3 (the ratio).** I agree with the plan's band, [3.0, 4.0], for every scenario, and add
  that the ratio at R = 1000 m differs from the ratio at R = 100 m by less than 15 %.
* **A1-4 (motion).** Agreed with the plan, and the S3 crossings at 5 and 15 m/s lie inside the
  speed-0 crossing's bootstrap interval widened by 2 %.

  synthetic uniform placements (not simulator placements; seed 7): 0.0004, 0.029, 0.082,

* Literature, searched before this commit (two queries): analytical models of 802.11 broadcast
  with hidden terminals exist — a semi-Markov EDCA broadcast model with hidden terminals,
  Nakagami-m fading and distinct transmission, carrier-sense and interference ranges (IEEE
  Access, 2020, doi:10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3027550); a 2-D VANET hidden-terminal broadcast model
  validated in ns-3 (EURASIP JWCN 2019, doi:10.1186/s13638-019-1548-4); and for UAV swarms, a
  density-adaptive CSMA/CA for flight-status broadcast (FMAC, IEEE IoT Journal, 2021). None has
  been read in full yet; they must be before any A1 mechanism enters the paper. A1 claims no
  mechanism as new: what it adds is the comparison of the two frames under one channel.

## What this cannot show

ns-3's PHY abstraction, not a radio; one propagation model (free space + Nakagami, no ground
reflection, no antenna pattern, no body shadowing); no relaying (each node's neighbours are its
one-hop range); the sending source is the published one. The capacities stay simulated.

**What it means.** The design's gain over signing every record, ×3.5, does not depend on how the
swarm is spread: from equal power to a 1 km disc where 11 % of the nodes that can spoil a frame
cannot hear it coming, the ratio stays within 3.46–3.58. What spreading changes is what
"capacity" counts. A receiver at R = 1 000 m has 8.7 neighbours (within 215 m) at the crossing
but 15.5 nodes able to spoil a frame there: it contends with nodes it does not count, and hidden
stations are a minority of them — my A1-2 mechanism, which the plan's "because hidden terminals
add collisions" overstates. Motion: only relative drift up to 4 m/s was tested; whether to test
the plan's 5–15 m/s as relative motion is a new scenario and a decision (OPEN_ITEMS H8).
