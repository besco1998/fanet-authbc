# A15 — a post-quantum signature in the frame (Falcon-512)

*Registered 2026-10-20 before any ns-3 run of cells `PQ1`/`PQ4` and before any Falcon timing.
Plan: `docs/AUTHBC Revision Plan.md`, A15. Outcome appended below a line at the end.*

## What is computed and run

* **Frames (emitted, computed before this commit).** The lean codec with a 666 B signature
  (Falcon-padded-512; liboqs 0.16.0 `OQS_SIG_falcon_padded_512_length_signature` = 666;
  plain Falcon-512 is variable up to 752 B): one record **748.8 B**, four records **776.0 B =
  194.0 B per record** (−74.1 % against per-record Falcon). `results/raw/pq_capacity_predictions.csv`.
* **Capacity.** ns-3 crossing search, cells `PQ1` (749 B, 50 frames/s) and `PQ4` (776 B,
  12.5 /s), 30 seeds, the published scenario and source, the seven registered node counts
  (`experiments/pq/plan.txt`); scored by `analysis/pq_capacity.py --score`.
* **Timing.** liboqs 0.16.0 `speed_sig` (built from the release tag with Falcon and ML-DSA-44
  only) on this host now and on pi-B when it is on (open item H4). Host figures are never
  reported as the board's.
* **Hybrid (arithmetic, not an emitted frame).** Ed25519 on every frame (the design, 173 B) and a
  Falcon signature over the chain head every k frames (+666 B and ≈ 2 B of length prefix):
  (173k + 668)/(4k) B per record = 76.7, 60.0, 49.9, 46.6 at k = 5, 10, 25, 50.

## Predictions — the plan's, verbatim

> an equation (6) estimate gives about 12 nodes with per-record Falcon and about 45 with the
> design. That is a ratio near 3.9×, **larger than with Ed25519**, because batching amortizes a
> much bigger signature. If confirmed, this turns a limitation into a positive finding: batching
> matters more in the post-quantum era.

Scored as: (1) ns-3 crossings within ±10 % of 12 and 45; (2) ns-3 ratio PQ4/PQ1 within ±0.3 of
3.9; (3) ns-3 ratio larger than the Ed25519 ratio of the paper (124.55 / 35.26 = 3.53) by more
than its interval allows (both crossing intervals combined).

## Mine `[agent]`

* **A15-1.** Each ns-3 crossing lies in the access-rule model's ±3 % band (a test of the model
  at frames 1.3 times longer than any it has met).
* **A15-2 (disputes the plan's point).** The ratio is **not larger** than with Ed25519: ns-3's
  PQ4/PQ1 lies within ±0.25 of 3.53. The signature dwarfs the records in both frames, so the
  design's frame is only 3 % longer than per-record Falcon's (against 12 % for Ed25519); but at
  these lengths a frame spends proportionally less time in the 8 µs detection window, which
  the airtime line, fitted on shorter frames, does not see. Prediction (2) is expected to fail,
  and (3) with it.
* **A15-3.** Falcon still fits where ML-DSA does not: 776 B is under the 1 500 B MTU, and the
  capacity of the design with Falcon (≈ 43) is above the per-record Ed25519 baseline's (35.3).

## What this cannot show

Falcon's constant-time signing on a flight controller (its floating-point sampler is the
known difficulty) — the timing here is liboqs on a PC, then a Pi. The hybrid is arithmetic.

## Outcome, capacity (2026-10-20; 420 ns-3 runs)

`results/raw/pq_capacity.csv` (`analysis/pq_capacity.py --score`).

| | ns-3 crossing [95 %] | access-rule model | Eq. (6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| per-record Falcon `PQ1` (749 B, 50 /s) | **12.41** [12.35, 12.46] | 12.34 (+0.6 %) | 11.56 (+7.4 %) |
| design `PQ4` (776 B, 12.5 /s) | **43.50** [42.92, 44.08] | 43.23 (+0.6 %) | 44.90 (−3.1 %) |
| ratio | **3.51** [3.45, 3.57] | 3.50 | 3.88 |

* Plan (1), crossings within ±10 % of 12 and 45: **HELD** (+3.4 %, −3.3 %).
* Plan (2), ratio within ±0.3 of 3.9: **FAILED** (3.51).
* Plan (3), ratio larger than with Ed25519 (3.53 [3.51, 3.56]): **FAILED** — the two are the same.
* A15-1, both crossings in the model's ±3 % band: **HELD** (+0.6 % each), at frames 1.3 times
  longer than any the model had met. Eq. (6) leaves its own ±6 % band at `PQ1` (+7.4 %): it is
  outside the range it was fitted on, as stated beforehand.
* A15-2, ratio within ±0.25 of 3.53: **HELD**. A15-3: **HELD** (776 B < 1 500 B; 43.5 > 35.3).

**What it means.** Batching does not matter *more* with a post-quantum signature: it matters
the same, ×3.5, because the gain comes from sending a quarter as many frames, and the frame
count does not care how large the signature is. What changes is that Falcon *fits*: the design
signed with Falcon-512 serves 43 nodes, more than per-record Ed25519 serves today (35), at 194 B
a record. The paper's "post-quantum signatures do not fit" was a statement about ML-DSA only.
Timing of Falcon sign and verify is still to be measured (on a quiet host, and on pi-B — H4).

## Outcome, timing (2026-10-22)

liboqs 0.16.0 `speed_sig` (Release; Falcon and ML-DSA-44 only; `OQS_OPT_TARGET=auto`; the same
build options on both machines), three repetitions of 10 s per operation, pinned to one core;
the median and the range of the three. `results/hw/pq_speed_sig.<host>.csv`
(`analysis/pq_timing.py`; raw logs beside them).

* **pi-B:** 1.8 GHz, flags 0x0, run after A7's flood.
* **Host:** i5-14400F, quiet (load 0.75–1.0, no ns-3 or SIH).

| µs | pi-B sign | pi-B verify | host sign | host verify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Falcon-padded-512 (the frame's) | **638** [638, 640] | **88.2** [88.1, 88.3] | 157 [155, 157] | 28.2 [28.1, 28.4] |
| Falcon-512 | 641 | 88.4 | 155 | 28.2 |
| ML-DSA-44 | 375 | 131 | 58.3 | 19.7 |
| Ed25519, pi-B (`cryptography`, July; 262.6 verify today) | 87.8 | 258.7 | | |

At the design's rates on pi-B:

* a sender signs 12.5 frames/s with Falcon in **0.8 % of a core**;
* a receiver of 124 neighbours verifies 1 550 frames/s in **13.7 % of a core** (Ed25519: 40 %).

No timing was predicted, so nothing is scored.

**What it means.** On a Raspberry Pi 4, Falcon-512 verifies three times faster than Ed25519
and signs seven times slower. Neither cost limits the design. Falcon's price is airtime (A15's
capacity part: 43.5 nodes, not 124), not computation.

**What this cannot show** (as registered): Falcon's constant-time signing on a flight
controller. This is liboqs on a Cortex-A72 with a floating-point unit.
