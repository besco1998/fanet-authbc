\chapter{Reproducibility, Defects and Research Integrity}\label{ch:reproducibility}

\needswork{STATUS: DRAFTED. This chapter is unusual for a thesis and deliberate. It reports the
errors found in this project's own results. Examiners should read it as the methodological
contribution it is intended to be, not as an admission appended to a limitations section.}

\section{Why this chapter exists}

Most theses report what was found. This one also reports \emph{what was found to be wrong, and how}.
The justification is not confession but transferability: the defects catalogued below are not
peculiar to this work, and several of them survived safeguards that are widely considered
sufficient --- version control, a green test suite, seeded runs, committed raw data.

By August 2026 ten results in this project had moved after they were first recorded. Only four
were catchable by running more seeds. Two were \emph{protected by passing tests}. Three were
contradictions between a paper and the artifact that generated it.

In October an external review of the paper found five more, and they were of a kind the first ten
had not prepared the project for (\Cref{sec:october}). Every one of them had survived two full
audits and a green suite of thirteen hundred tests, and one of them was a ``correction'' the
second audit had itself introduced.

\begin{remark}[The load-bearing observation]
\textbf{More seeds fixes only C1.} C2--C9 are immune to sample size. ``We ran thirty seeds'' is
therefore not a statement of correctness, and the discipline installed after the C1 failures gave
no protection at all against the C2 failures found later --- nor did the discipline installed
after those protect against C7.
\end{remark}

\section{October 2026: five errors that survived two audits}\label{sec:october}

\begin{description}
\item[The design had never been built.] The configuration the results headlined --- delta
records, one signature per four --- was a \emph{sum of sizes}: a record size from one module, a
header measured on frames of a different encoding from another, and a signature length. No frame
carrying delta records had been encoded, sent or decoded, and the receiver that would decode one
did not exist. Each number was individually correct, so re-deriving any one of them reproduced it.

\item[Its verifiability was wrong.] The byte model amortised one keyframe over four frames; the
verifiability model treated the four as independent. At its own loss budget the design verified
$\lossThFour$ of its records, not $0.95$ (\Cref{thm:t3prime}).

\item[The exclusion test left out two things a frame must carry] --- the chain link and a record
that decodes alone --- and counted seven data rates where the standard defines twelve. The
August audit had ``corrected'' the exclusion from four rates to three on the strength of a leaner
header; with the link charged, that correction is itself wrong (\Cref{sec:excl-history}).

\item[A record size was used at the wrong time scale.] The delta record measured with records
$50$\,ms apart was used on the low-rate arm, where they are seconds apart and the same record is
half as large again (\Cref{sec:perframe}).

\item[A source was cited under the wrong authors,] in the paper, the thesis, the code and eight
documents, for two months. Each citation had been ``verified'': the DOI resolved and the claim
was in the PDF. Nobody had compared the author list with the title page --- and the paper carried
a paragraph asserting that every citation had been checked.
\end{description}

The first four are one error seen four times. What fixed them was not a better audit but an
object: the frame format was implemented, with a sender and a receiver, and the byte, loss and
exclusion results were recomputed \emph{from one definition of a frame}, whose arithmetic a test
holds equal to the frames the code emits. The fifth was fixed by a script that compares every
bibliography entry with its registry record, and by deleting the paragraph: a claim of that kind
belongs in a check that runs, not in prose.

\subsection{The capacity ceiling, and a prediction that failed}

The same review asked for confidence intervals on the 802.11 capacities. Producing them meant
simulating each configuration directly instead of applying one load ceiling, and the prediction
that the two would agree within $\pm 10\%$ --- committed before the runs --- failed
(\Cref{ch:validation}). The ratios this thesis had described as ``protected by construction''
moved with it.

Chasing the per-run spread of those simulations produced the 802.11 counterpart of an artifact
already known on the low-rate arm (below): strictly periodic senders keep their relative phases
for a whole run, so a run is one phase configuration, and in an unlucky one a few nodes lose
every frame while the rest lose none. Three follow-up experiments were registered before they
were run. Two of their predictions failed and are reported as failures; the third, that the
choice of source moves the mean by less than a tolerance set in advance, held; with all six
cells run, the difference is small, consistently of one sign, and not yet explained
(\Cref{sec:capacity}).

\section{A simulator artifact large enough to change what a reader should believe}

The ns-3 LoRaWAN module's periodic sender fires on an exact interval, and LoRaWAN ALOHA has no
backoff, so relative transmission phases are frozen for an entire run: a pair that collides once
collides on every cycle. Delivery becomes \emph{bimodal} instead of noisy about a mean. Over 30
seeds at $N{=}5$, 22 runs deliver exactly $1.000$ and the rest fall to $0.20$--$0.84$. The
coefficient of variation is inflated \textbf{2--8$\times$} relative to a sender with one-second
transmission jitter, the inflation growing with node count ($2.9\times$ at $N{=}5$, $7.9\times$
at $N{=}100$). The LoRaWAN specification makes it a rule that an end-device ``pseudo-randomly
changes its transmit periodicity to prevent systematic synchronization of populations of
end-device transmissions''~\cite{lorawan_ts001}; the module's sender does not.

\textbf{The measurement is claimed, not the observation.} Durand and Booysen report the same
effect in their own results, attributing bimodal delivery to nodes that ``always transmit on a
specific SF, time, and channel'', producing ``certain packet collisions being repeated for every
transmission cycle''~\cite{durand2025loramesh}. What is added here is the quantification.

On 802.11 carrier sense softens the effect and does not remove it. The simulator declares the
medium busy only at the end of a $4$\,\si{\micro\second} preamble-detection period; two
strictly periodic nodes whose phases fall inside that window collide in every period. At
$N = \phaseNodes$ the per-run standard deviation of delivery is \phaseSdFrozen{} with strictly
periodic sources and \phaseSdRedrawn{} when phases are redrawn every period. The means are the
same: a difference of \srcMeanDiff{} seen in the first thirty seeds did not replicate on fresh
ones (\srcFreshD{} $\pm$ \srcFreshSe).

\subsection{A claim that was withdrawn}

It had been intended to report that this dispersion is undetectable in the ns-3 LoRa literature
because that literature does not state replication information, and a survey was pre-registered
to test it --- inclusion criteria, keyword set and a $25\%$ falsification threshold, all committed
before the corpus was assembled. At the achieved $n{=}23$ the result is $21.7\%$ reporting with a
$95\%$ interval of $[7.5, 43.7]\%$. \textbf{That interval contains the threshold, so the survey
cannot answer its own question, and no claim about the literature is made.} The protocol, corpus
and adjudications are in the repository as a null result.

\section{An automated staleness gate, and what it does not cover}

Every model-derived artifact is re-derived from current code on every run and compared row by row;
a single differing value fails the build. This makes silent staleness of \emph{derived} data
impossible.

It does not make staleness impossible. Two limits were found the hard way:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scope.} Every drift guard originally parsed the paper. The specification documents
were outside that scope, so corrections propagated into the paper and not into the documents for
three weeks --- including a table built verbatim on data the project had already purged and
superseded.
\item \textbf{Direction.} Retracting a finding in a register does not remove it from the prose. One
retracted claim was found alive in the paper months later, one hundred lines below a sentence
stating the opposite, and later again in the open-items register. \textbf{When you retract, grep the
whole repository.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Practices that earned their place}

\begin{description}
\item[Pre-registration, committed data-free.] Predictions are committed as their own commit before
data exist, so the ordering is third-party checkable. This cost the project a claim once: an
assertion of pre-registration was \emph{withdrawn} rather than reconstructed, because writing an
expectations file after results are known manufactures evidence.

\item[Stating the expected value before measuring.] This is what caught the $2.4$\,GHz
basic-rate artifact, which repeatability would never have caught.

\item[Retaining a discarded model as a labelled failure curve.] The naive broadcast reduction
remains in the codebase, clearly marked, so the $16\times$ error it produces can be shown rather
than described.

\item[Keeping per-seed raw data, not summaries.] This allowed validation bands to be re-derived at
thirty seeds without re-simulating.

\item[An independent second implementation.] The slot-exact simulator, written before the published
model was found, shares none of its assumptions and is what turns agreement into evidence.

\item[No typed results.] Since October 2026 every result in the paper, and every corrected result
in this thesis, is a macro in a file that a script writes from the artifacts. A sentence that
states a verdict instead of a number --- ``eight of twelve'', ``does not meet the target'' --- is
held by the same script, which refuses to run if the artifact no longer says so.

\item[A check on the bibliography that runs.] Every entry is compared with its Crossref, DataCite
or arXiv record --- family names in order, initials, title, year --- and entries with no registry
record must name the held document they were checked against.
\end{description}

\section{Two defects worth stating individually}

\subsection{A test that asserted a bug}

A utilisation function returned exactly zero for a single node, with a unit test asserting that a
lone sender never contends. The function conflated \emph{contention} with \emph{utilisation}: a lone
sender does not collide but still occupies airtime, and returning zero declared a single node able
to carry unbounded traffic. The defect was harmless until another result began reporting
single-node cases --- \textbf{and it was protected by a passing test that had encoded the wrong
behaviour as intended behaviour}.

\subsection{A criterion that could not fail}

The project pre-registered a success criterion requiring at least a $40\%$ reduction in
authentication bytes, and committed it to version control two days before the result existed. The
ordering is genuine and verifiable.

The quantity it tests, however, reduces exactly to $1 - 1/b$. The threshold is therefore
$b \geq 2$ --- independent of encoding, scheme, placement, header size and signature size. The
companion condition on verifiability is satisfied by construction with zero margin. \textbf{No
configuration that batches at all could have failed the test.}

The pre-registration is real and worth keeping; what had to go was the implication that it
constituted a risk the design might not have survived.

\section{What a reader should take from this}

Three times before October 2026 a claim in this project survived because two facts were each
individually recorded and never placed side by side, and this chapter used to end: a register
that stores facts separately does not compose them; \emph{only re-derivation does}.

That was not enough. The errors of \Cref{sec:october} were re-derived, repeatedly, and reproduced
every time, because each part was right. \textbf{What composes facts is building the thing they
describe and making it run.} A design that exists only as a sum has no receiver to refuse a frame
it cannot decode, and no encoder to refuse a frame that does not fit.
