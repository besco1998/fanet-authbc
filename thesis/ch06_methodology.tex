\chapter{Experimental Methodology}\label{ch:methodology}

This chapter states how every result of \Cref{ch:bytes}--\Cref{ch:lowrate} was obtained: the
statistical rules, the telemetry the measurements run on, the two simulation scenarios, and the
hardware with its uncertainty.

\section{Binding statistical rules}\label{sec:rules}

Every experiment obeys the following, and where a result predates a rule that is recorded rather
than retrofitted:

\begin{itemize}
\item At least $30$ seeded repetitions per configuration, with seeds logged in the artifact.
\item Report median and bootstrap $95\%$ confidence interval over $10^4$ resamples.
\item Microbenchmarks: $\geq 10^4$ iterations after $10^3$ warm-up, garbage collection disabled,
      runs spanning a suspend discarded.
\item Comparisons state effect size; never bare means.
\item Raw artifacts are immutable once frozen.
\item Any model-versus-measurement deviation above $10\%$ must be investigated and either explained
      in writing or the model corrected. \textbf{Silent tolerance widening is forbidden.}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why 30 seeds, and what it does not buy}

The 30-seed rule is not arbitrary and is not original policy --- it was imposed \emph{after} four
headline numbers were found to have been distorted by small-sample means evaluated against
thresholds. None was a modelling error; every one was sampling.

\begin{remark}[The limit of the rule]
More seeds fixes exactly one class of defect: a small-sample mean landing on the wrong side of a
threshold. It does nothing for a threshold applied to a mean rather than a distribution, for an
unverified constant on the measurement path, for a configuration change that perturbs the random
realisation, or for a claim wider than the experiment supports. \Cref{ch:reproducibility}
catalogues instances of each, several found in this project's own work \emph{after} the 30-seed
rule was in force.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Thresholds must be applied to distributions}

A criterion of the form $V \geq 0.95$ is a \emph{reliability} requirement. Applying it to a mean
across seeds answers a throughput question instead. Where this thesis reports a threshold crossing
it reports the distribution alongside --- minimum, maximum, standard deviation, and the count of
seeds that individually fail --- and where the mean and per-realisation readings disagree, both are
given.

\section{Pre-registration}

Where a run tests a prediction, the prediction is committed to version control \emph{before the
data exist}, as its own commit, so the ordering is third-party checkable in the repository history.

This practice is enforced rather than aspirational, and it has cost the project a claim: one
pre-registration assertion was \emph{withdrawn} rather than reconstructed, because writing an
expectations file after results are known manufactures evidence. It has also exposed a subtler
defect in this work's own practice, recorded in \Cref{ch:validation}: a pre-registered
\emph{directional} prediction that the experiment had no statistical power to resolve.

\Cref{app:prereg} lists every registration with its commit, what it predicted and what
happened. Of its eighteen entries, seven failed in whole or in part, one could not be scored and
one was met by a criterion that could not have failed; one has been run with two boards of the
two to five it covers. They are in the table with the rest.

\section{Telemetry}\label{sec:telemetry}

A record holds nine integers: a millisecond timestamp; latitude and longitude in units of
$10^{-7}$ degree; altitude in centimetres; three velocity components in centimetres per second;
battery level in percent; and a flight mode. Integers and not floating point, because a
canonical encoding must give the same bytes on every platform.

Records come from a seeded generator. A vehicle starts at a random position with a horizontal
speed of up to $5$\,m/s in each axis; at every step its velocity changes by at most
$0.1$\,m/s per axis, its position integrates that velocity with a jitter of two units, its
battery falls by one percent with probability $0.02$ and its mode changes with probability
$0.01$; the timestamp advances by $50 \pm 3$\,ms. Every size in this thesis that is not
marked otherwise is measured over thirty seeds of one thousand such records.

Two properties of the generator limit what its sizes mean, and both are checked.
\emph{Its records are $50$\,ms apart}, the spacing of the $20$~records/s operating point it
was written for, while the adopted point is $50$~records/s. For the lean format that changes
nothing: the delta record is already at the floor of the format at $50$\,ms, nine bytes for
nine fields, and it is the same nine bytes when the generator is run at $20$\,ms
(\Cref{sec:e6}). \emph{It is synthetic.} \Cref{sec:e6} sets it against twelve public flight
logs at the spacing those logs allow, and against an autopilot run in simulation at $20$\,ms.

\section{Simulation campaign}

Two scenarios are used, for two different questions. The \emph{saturation} scenario validates the
channel models: IEEE 802.11a ad-hoc at $\SI{6}{\mega\bit\per\second}$, RTS/CTS off, with $N$
saturated senders in \emph{both} unicast and broadcast modes, frame sizes taken from the real
framer output. The \emph{delivery} scenario measures capacity: the same channel with each node
offering one frame per period, so the network runs at a chosen load and the delivered fraction is
read at the receivers. The capacities of \Cref{ch:codesign} come from the second, with the send
instant redrawn in every period for the reasons given in \Cref{sec:capacity}. Transport is a packet socket rather than a flow monitor, because MAC-level
goodput with no addressing artifacts is what the models predict.

\paragraph{How a capacity is read.} A configuration is a frame size and a frame rate per node.
It is simulated over a grid of neighbourhood sizes $N$, thirty seeds per point, twenty seconds
per run, and the delivered fraction of a run is the number of frames received over the number
sent times $N-1$. $\Nmax$ is the largest simulated $N$ whose mean delivered fraction is at
least $0.95$ with every smaller simulated $N$ also passing. Its interval is the $2.5$--$97.5$
percentile of that quantity over ten thousand bootstrap resamples of the seeds. A second reading
is given beside it wherever the two differ: the largest $N$ at which at least $95\%$ of the
individual runs reach $0.95$.

\begin{remark}[The comparison rule]
Each mode is compared \emph{only} to its matching analytic variant: unicast to the acknowledged DCF
model, broadcast to the published broadcast model. Mixing them is the single most effective way to
fabricate a model gap, and this project did exactly that for several phases before catching it.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The energy rig}

Energy is measured outside the board. An INA219 current and voltage sensor with a
$0.1\,\Omega$ shunt sits in the board's $5$\,V supply and is read fifty times a second by a
microcontroller, which has no operating system to perturb the schedule and adds no load to the
device under test. Each reading is one conversion of about half a millisecond --- a sample, not
an average --- so the mean over a window is the mean power only if the load does not repeat in
step with the sampling; a loop that runs back to back does not. The board marks the start and end of each measurement window on a GPIO line
that the microcontroller records with every sample, so the samples that belong to a window are
known without aligning two clocks.

A measurement is a pair of sixty-second windows: one idle, one in which the board runs the
operation in a tight loop and counts its completions $n$. With $\bar P$ the mean power of a
window,
\[
E_{\mathrm{op}} = \frac{(\bar P_{\mathrm{load}} - \bar P_{\mathrm{idle}})\; t_{\mathrm{load}}}{n} .
\]
Each configuration is measured five times and the median is reported with the range. The board
is checked for throttling before and after every window --- by the firmware's flags and, since
October 2026, by the kernel's count of under-voltage events, which still moves once the flags
are set --- and a flagged window is not used. Before a session the rig itself is put through a
fixed sequence (\texttt{hw/rig\_check.py}): the voltage reading must not move with the state of
the marking line, the supply must hold with every core busy, and the board must log no
under-voltage. The captures of July pass the meter's part of that test; a rig that fails is not
measured on. The harness prints the value it \emph{expects} before it reads the meter, so that a
measurement cannot be quietly fitted to expectation.

\subsection{The model the meter is compared with}

The model composes separately measured times with two measured powers: $P_c = 0.749$\,W, the
incremental power of the processor while it computes, and $P_r = 0.218$\,W, that of the radio
while it receives. Per record, for one signature over a frame of $b$ records,
\[
E = \underbrace{P_c\Bigl(t_{\mathrm{enc}} + t_h + \frac{t_{\mathrm{sg}}}{b}\Bigr)}_{\text{sender}}
  + \underbrace{P_c\Bigl(\frac{t_v}{b} + t_h\Bigr) + P_r\,\frac{T_{\mathrm{air}}}{b}}_{\text{receiver}} ,
\]
with $t_{\mathrm{enc}}$ the time to encode a record, $t_h$ to hash it, $t_{\mathrm{sg}}$ and
$t_v$ to sign and verify, and $T_{\mathrm{air}}$ the airtime of the frame. Only the sender's
term is metered (\Cref{tab:energy}). The model reached this form through two corrections that
the meter forced. It had no hashing term, although every record is hashed once by the sender to
extend the chain and once by the receiver to check it. And its processor power had been taken
as the median over isolated primitives, $0.634$\,W; metered on four composed pipelines the
power is $0.749$\,W, the four agreeing within $3.8\%$, so one constant is adequate; the
composed value is $18\%$ above the earlier one. Two of those four pipelines are the rows of
\Cref{tab:energy}. The power in the model is thus the median of the very runs it is set beside:
what that table tests is the composition of the times, and it cannot test the power.

The radio's power is the weakest of the model's inputs and enters only the figures that are
labelled as modelled. $P_r$ is the power the \emph{receiving board} added while a saturated
unicast stream arrived on the $2.4$\,GHz band: two repetitions of thirty seconds, $0.205$ and
$0.231$\,W. It therefore includes the processor's share of receiving and the acknowledgements
that radio sent, and it is applied here to broadcast frames on $5$\,GHz, which are not
acknowledged. It has not been measured in the configuration it is used for.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent Three consequences follow. The \emph{ratios} this thesis reports --- the batched
sender against the per-record one, and the meter against the model --- do not depend on the
sensor's gain, because the model's power constant was measured with the same sensor: they carry
about one percent, from drift and repeatability. The gap between meter and model, $7.5$ to
$12.3\%$, is therefore several times the uncertainty of its measurement and is a property of the
prototype (\Cref{ch:codesign}). The \emph{absolute} values in microjoules carry the sensor's
gain error in addition, and that was not measured: the calibration against a known load that
the procedure prescribes was not recorded for these runs. They should be read as accurate to
the sensor's factory tolerance and no better. One partial check exists. In October 2026 the
baseline row was metered again on the second board through the second sensor and came out
\enCtlVsJuly\% from July's figure (\Cref{tab:energy}). Two sensors with a gross error in one of
them would not agree so; two sensors sharing an error --- the same nominal shunt, the same
calibration word --- would, and this does not exclude it. The row that could not be reported in
July, with JSON records, was metered again there with ten repetitions and no window disturbed:
\enJsonMeas\,\si{\micro\joule} \enJsonRange{} per record.

One configuration is not reported. In three of its five repetitions the idle window read up to
$0.5$\,W above the others --- a background process on the board --- which makes the difference
meaningless. It is listed as unusable and is to be measured again.

\subsection{Two radios}

Two boards in ad hoc mode on a $5$\,GHz channel. With one sending broadcast frames and one
counting them, they measure the loss of a link with no contention, and the time from one frame
of a saturated sender to its next, which the receiver reads from the sequence numbers it spans
and its own clock. With both sending and both counting they measure the loss among stations
that contend, at a set load or saturated; each sender then draws its send instant within every
period, as the simulations do. The chip's frame-burst mode is read from the firmware and logged
before and after every session. \Cref{sec:hardware} reports what these experiments do and do
not support.
