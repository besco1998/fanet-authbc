\chapter{Conclusions, Limitations and Future Work}\label{ch:conclusions}

\section{What this thesis establishes}

\begin{enumerate}
\item After compression, authentication --- not data --- is the dominant on-air cost of a
tamper-evident UAV telemetry ledger. At the smallest encoding studied, the signature is larger than
the record it signs.

\item Frame-level self-batching is the correct placement, and it Pareto-dominates block-level
aggregation under \emph{any} stationary loss process, not merely under the independent-loss
assumption the emulator implements.

\item On IEEE 802.11 the freshness deadline binds before the frame size does, so the amplification
law that motivates aggressive compression is never operative: compression pays exactly $1\times$.
On low-rate links the frame size binds and the leverage is real. \textbf{The regimes are different
in kind.}

\item A delta-coded frame that depends on its predecessor does not meet a verifiability target
equal to the loss rate. Every frame of the design therefore carries a self-contained record, and
the cost of that is small.

\item \exclCountCap{} of the twelve EU863-870 data rates cannot carry one signed, hash-chained
frame: five because a $64$\,B signature does not fit a $51$\,B payload, three because header,
chain link and signature fill a $115$\,B payload. The result holds under four stated conditions.

\item Built as one frame format and simulated directly, the design carries \bprLeanDesign\,B per
record where per-record signing carries \bprLeanBase\,B (\saveLean\% fewer), and sustains
\nmaxLeanDesign{} nodes at 95\% delivery where per-record signing sustains \nmaxLeanBase, at
an operating point compliant with 3GPP TS~22.125.

\item Of the four design axes, placement and batching interact exactly, by $\ga(1-1/b)$, and in
the frame as built delta coding is conditional on batching as well: both gains are zero at
$b = 1$. The scheme axis is byte-neutral. The four-axis co-design claim, as originally stated,
was overstated; what is true is that batching is the axis the other gains depend on.

\item The gain does not rest on the conditions it was first computed under. Spread over a
$1$\,km disc with hidden stations, moving at up to $15$\,m/s, carried by six 802.11 PHYs and
rates, or signed with Falcon-512, the design serves \phySimRatioLo--\phySimRatioHi{} times the
nodes of per-record signing (\Cref{ch:codesign}). What changes is the batch a deadline admits,
which the design map gives for any record rate and deadline.

\item The protocol's properties are proven in Tamarin, in three models, and two defences close
two of the gaps the first threat model left: a window on GNSS time refuses frames an attacker
holds back, and an $8$\,B group MAC stops floods from outside the swarm. A flood from a member is
not stopped, and is stated (\Cref{ch:sysmodel,ch:codesign}).

\item The prototype's two anomalies were the interpreter's. Compiled, one receiver core serves
\rxRustNodes{} nodes, so the channel binds again, and the design's sender spends
\enRustMeas\,\si{\micro\joule} per record, \enFirstOverRust{} times less than the record-linked format.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The research questions, answered}\label{sec:rq-answers}

\begin{description}
\item[RQ1 --- how large is the overhead, and where does it dominate?] The authentication
fraction is $\ga/(s+\ga)$ and passes one half exactly when the record is no larger than the
signature (\Cref{thm:t1}). With $64$\,B signatures that is where compact encodings put a
telemetry record: $49\%$ for CBOR and $59\%$ for delta coding, and $96$ of $109$\,B once the
chain link inside the record is counted as authentication material (\Cref{ch:bytes}).

\item[RQ2 --- which placement?] One signature over the records of one frame. It verifies with
probability $1-p$ whatever the batch, where a signed unit spread over $n$ frames verifies with
$(1-p)^n$ and is infeasible beyond one frame once $\varepsilon \leq p$
(\Cref{thm:t3,thm:t3c}); and the frame must also decode alone (\Cref{thm:t3prime}).
Self-batching needs one ordinary signature. Cross-signer aggregation is needed only for
relayed records, which this thesis models and does not evaluate.

\item[RQ3 --- when does an aggregatable scheme win?] Not in any of the eighty cells evaluated
at $6$\,Mb/s. For a node's own records BLS costs bytes; for relayed ones it pays only where the
radio draws more than thirty times the power of the CPU, against a measured ratio of $0.29$, and
one core cannot verify it above $200$ records per second (\Cref{ch:codesign}). Its regime is
links on which airtime is orders of magnitude dearer than computation.

\item[RQ4 --- the end-to-end configuration, and do the models survive?] Delta coding, Ed25519,
one signature and one chain link per frame, four records, a self-contained record at the head of
every frame: \bprLeanDesign\,B per record against \bprLeanBase\,B, and \nmaxLeanDesign{} nodes
against \nmaxLeanBase. The saturation model of the broadcast channel survives simulation, within
\valBcast\%. The single utilisation ceiling built on it did not, and was replaced by direct
simulation; a model of the access rule alone then reproduced the simulated capacities within
\modelWorst\% and predicted \modelPredCases{} unseen ones within \modelPredWorst\%. On two
radios the access rule was observed within $6\%$ where contention can be isolated; a registered
prediction of their loss below saturation held at two loads and failed at the third; no
capacity was measured (\Cref{ch:codesign,ch:validation}).

\item[RQ5 --- where can the frame not be sent at all?] On \exclCount{} of the twelve
EU863-870 LoRaWAN data rates, under five conditions, the fifth being this frame format for
\exclFmtCount{} of them; a $48$\,B signature, a $13$\,B tag or a
chain link kept off the air each recover some of them, and \Cref{tab:t6relax} says which
(\Cref{ch:lowrate}).
\end{description}

\section{Limitations}

\subsection{Modelling scope}

The capacities are computed in one collision domain of static nodes. A separate scenario spread
the nodes over discs of up to $1$\,km, with capture, Nakagami fading and hidden stations, and
moved them on independent paths at up to $15$\,m/s (\Cref{sec:spread-swarm}): the gain held, and
what a capacity counts changed. That scenario has one propagation model, no Doppler and no
time-correlated fading. On the low-rate arm mobility moves collision-limited delivery by nothing
measurable (\Cref{sec:lora-mobility}); that is not a statement that mobility is harmless to a
link. Loss in the verifiability experiments is generated at the frame level --- independent,
bursty, or growing with frame length --- and collision loss was traced in the simulator; no loss
was measured on air. Falcon-512 was timed and simulated in the frame; signatures that do not fit
one frame remain projections.

\subsection{Validation scope}

On hardware, two radios gave the loss of a link, the access rule between two saturated
stations, and a failed prediction of the loss between two busy ones (\Cref{sec:hardware}); a
figure for airtime that earlier versions carried is withdrawn there. No capacity was measured,
and the capacities of \Cref{ch:codesign} rest on simulation, of one collision domain in which
every station hears every other. Unequal received power and capture were simulated and raise
them by about an eighth (\Cref{sec:spread}); hidden stations and motion were simulated
separately (\Cref{sec:spread-swarm}). Record sizes come from a synthetic generator, checked
against twelve public flight logs at $0.2$\,s spacing and above and, at the $20$\,ms of the
operating point, against flights of an autopilot in simulation, whose navigation-quality fields
do not vary (\Cref{tab:airframes}); no flight log recorded at $50$\,Hz was found. Only the
sender's energy is metered, with a sensor never checked against a reference load: interpreted,
the frame-linked sender costs \enLeanOverFirst{} times the record-linked format's energy per record; compiled,
\enFirstOverRust{} times less. The record-linked format's composed energy is a lower bound by roughly
$7$--$14\%$, the residual being uncharged prototype framing overhead. The receiver-CPU ceilings
charge cryptography only; the interpreted decoder costs several times more and the compiled one
does not, timed without a radio or a dispatcher in the path (\Cref{ch:codesign}). Capacity was
simulated on six 802.11 PHYs and rates and on no other family of link, and for batches other
than one record and four only as a test of the capacity rule (\Cref{tab:batchdim}): nothing
else was evaluated at them.

\subsection{The low-rate arm}

Analysis and simulation only: no radio metered, no energy column, one spreading factor per run.
The capacity figure is a within-one-spreading-factor bound, not a network capacity, and its
confidence interval is a knife edge.

\subsection{The boundary itself}

The exclusion is conditional: on LoRaWAN's regional payload limits, on a public-key signature in
every frame, on no loss recovery, on a verifiability target no looser than the loss rate, and,
for \exclFmtCount{} of its \exclCount{} data rates, on the frame format of this thesis. It was
also stated wrongly twice before reaching its present form (\Cref{sec:excl-history}), and
narrowed a third time when a review of 2026-10-09 separated what belongs to the problem from
what belongs to the format; a reader is entitled to weigh that. What has not moved in any version is the part that rests on one
inequality: $64 > 51$.

\subsection{Security scope}

Confidentiality, availability and key management are outside the design (\Cref{sec:threat}). A
member of the swarm holding the group key can still flood, and a delayed frame is refused only
while GNSS time is held. The properties are proven in three models, not one, with canonical
encoding assumed injective (\Cref{sec:formal}). With one chain link per frame, continuity inside
a frame rests on the frame signature alone, and showing one record to a third party means
showing its frame. The receiver keeps every accepted frame for that purpose, which it did not
do until a review of 2026-10-09 (\Cref{sec:threat}).

\section{Future work}

\subsection{Small}

\begin{description}
\item[Pricing implicit certificates.] The certificate accounting charges an explicit certificate
periodically. Implicit (ECQV) certificates are the standard smaller alternative and were never
modelled. The current charge is an upper bound, which cuts against this work's own comparison ---
so the gap is safe but should be closed.

\item[Telemetry at the operating rate.] Public flight logs carry position at 5\,Hz, and the
delta record at 20\,ms spacing rests on the generator and on simulated flights of one autopilot.
A public log recorded at $50$\,Hz on a real vehicle, if one appears, would settle it.
\end{description}

\subsection{Substantial}

\begin{description}
\item[An analytical channel-access delay.] The constraint that \emph{sets} the batch counts fill
time and airtime. \Cref{tab:fresh} adds the channel-access delay as simulated at the
capacity point --- under a millisecond on average and \frChMax\,ms at worst, against a fill time
of \frFill\,ms --- so the batch is not affected; but the figure exists only where a simulation
was run. An access-delay term from the model of \Cref{sec:why} would let the constraint be
evaluated at any load.

\item[Multi-spreading-factor capacity on the low-rate arm.] Each run fixes one data rate,
forfeiting the quasi-orthogonality that supplies much of a real deployment's capacity. That answers
a different question --- deployment capacity rather than per-configuration feasibility --- and it
is the question an operator would ask next.

\item[The 5G NR sidelink.] Only 802.11 was simulated. The direct link between devices of the
cellular standard has a medium access of its own, which the contention model does not describe;
whether the frame count dominates there as it does here needs its own simulation.

\item[Post-quantum signatures that do not fit one frame.] Falcon-512 fits a frame and the gain
holds with it (\Cref{sec:phys}). An ML-DSA signature does not fit a frame at any batch, and the
deadline caps the batch, so it cannot be amortised either; it needs a variant of the design built
around fragmentation.
\end{description}

\subsection{Speculative}

The exclusion bound is link-agnostic, and three broadcast systems already in use have been put
through it (\Cref{sec:other-links}). Instantiating it on other constrained bands --- and on the
signature sizes the post-quantum standards actually mandate --- would say which of tomorrow's radio
and cryptographic combinations are feasible before either is deployed. That is the natural
generalisation of this work.

\section{Closing}

This thesis introduces no new cryptographic primitive and makes no claim to one. Its contribution
is a reproducible co-design of existing, standardised components; the quantified trade-offs that
co-design entails; the boundary beyond which no batch size helps, with its conditions; and an
account --- unusually explicit for a thesis --- of the errors made in establishing that boundary and
the classes of defect that survived the safeguards intended to prevent them.

The most durable result here is also the smallest one that survived scrutiny: on five EU863-870
data rates, a $64$-byte signature does not fit a $51$-byte payload, and no batch size, encoding
or placement changes that. A shorter authenticator does, and \Cref{ch:lowrate} says what it
buys.
