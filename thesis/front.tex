\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}

Flying ad-hoc networks (FANETs) stream telemetry records so small that a cryptographic signature
($64$--$96$\,B) exceeds the record it signs: compactly encoded, a record is \keyLean\,B, and
\deltaLean\,B as a difference from its predecessor. Once the payload has been
compressed, authentication --- not data --- becomes the dominant on-air cost, and the operational
question stops being \emph{how many bytes does a scheme save} and becomes \emph{which
configurations can run at all}.

This thesis answers the second question. It searches four design axes jointly --- record
\emph{encoding}, authentication \emph{placement}, signature \emph{scheme} and \emph{batch} size ---
against a byte model, an NS-3-validated IEEE 802.11 broadcast channel model, and energy measured
on Raspberry~Pi~4 hardware. Three results follow, ordered by how durable they are.

\textbf{An exclusion.} A frame that verifies alone must carry a header, a chain link, a
signature and one record that decodes without its predecessor. A link whose payload cannot hold
those admits no such telemetry at any batch size, and packet loss forecloses fragmentation as an
escape whenever the verifiability target is no looser than the loss rate. On LoRaWAN this removes
\exclCount{} of the twelve EU863-870 data rates: five because a $64$\,B signature alone overflows
a $51$\,B payload, and three because header, link and signature fill a $115$\,B payload before
any record. The conditions under which that holds, and what each relaxation buys, are stated with
it.

\textbf{Bytes.} Built as one frame format with a sender and a receiver, the design puts
\bprLeanDesign\,B per record on air where per-record signing puts \bprLeanBase\,B
(\saveLean\% fewer), at an operating point compliant with 3GPP TS~22.125. Every frame carries one
self-contained record: a frame that depends on its predecessor does not meet the verifiability
target at its own loss rate.

\textbf{Capacity.} Simulated directly, with a bootstrap interval, one 802.11a collision domain
of \nmaxLeanDesign{} nodes still delivers 95\% of frames with the design, against
\nmaxLeanBase{} nodes with per-record signing.

In the byte model the axes were first searched with, only placement and batching interact, by
exactly $\ga(1-1/b)$; in the frame as built, delta coding is conditional on batching too, since a
frame that must decode alone starts with a full record. The signature-scheme axis is byte-neutral
among $64$\,B schemes. AUTHBC
introduces no new cryptographic primitive. Its contribution is a reproducible co-design, its
quantified trade-offs, the boundary beyond which no batch size helps, with the conditions under
which it holds and what each relaxation buys --- and a
methodological record of the defects found while establishing it, including three results this
thesis had to correct after an external review.

\noindent\textbf{A note on $\Hf$.} It is a \emph{range}, not a constant. Canonical CBOR
encodes small integers in one byte and large ones in five, so the measured frame header moves by
six bytes with the magnitudes of the sender identifier and sequence number (\Cref{tab:hf}).
